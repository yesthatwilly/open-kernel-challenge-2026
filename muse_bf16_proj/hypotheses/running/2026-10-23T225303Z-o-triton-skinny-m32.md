# o-triton-skinny-m32

## Change

Extend the skinny Triton GEMM from pooler-triton-skinny-m32 to layer == 'o' at m <= 32 (n=8192, k=1024, 16.8 MB): in kernel_fn route o at m <= 32 to the same kernel with BLOCK_N=64 (128 programs), BLOCK_K=256 (only 4 K-steps, so num_stages=4 covers the whole loop), BLOCK_M from m, fp32 accumulate, bf16 store into torch.empty((m, 8192), bf16). o at m >= 128 and every other layer keep what they have. Kernel moved: nvjet_sm90_tst_64x8/64x16/64x32_64x16_2x1_v_bz_TNT, 4.5-5.1 us of GPU time inside a 6.6-7.0 us reported shape against a 3.5 us floor; a single launch already, so like unpooler-full this isolates bandwidth on a short-K weight where each program's stream is only 128 KB.

## Expected

- `layer=o load=decode_1 *` faster: 7.0 us reported, 5.1 us kernel on 16.8 MB (3.3 TB/s); target ~4 us kernel
- `layer=o load=decode_16 *` faster: 6.7 us, same
- `layer=o load=decode_32 *` faster: 6.6 us, same
- `layer=o load=verify_1 *` faster: m=8, 6.9 us, same
- `layer=o load=verify_16 *` flat: m=128 keeps cuBLAS; control

## Risk

Smallest absolute upside of the family (at most ~1.5 us per shape, four shapes), and the reported-minus-kernel gap of ~2 us is graph-node latency this change cannot touch, so expect a small effect that may sit inside the load CI; read the per-shape o rows. Short K (4 steps of 256) means a program's pipeline barely fills; BLOCK_K=128 with num_stages=8 is the ab-tune. Tests touched: m=33 o (stays on cuBLAS since 33 > 32). Same correctness and graph properties as the parent.

## Adversarial review

Reviewed 2026-10-24T040441Z by an adversarial Fable subagent (model fable).

**Verdict: proceed.** Interpretable in one run (only the four o m<=32 rows can move, each is a single Triton launch replacing a single nvjet launch, and the profile gives the kernel's own us so 16.8 MB / t is read directly), but the family's own measurements predict flat-to-loss on 3 of the 4 'faster' rows: the Triton stream has reached 3.3 TB/s (BLOCK_M=16) and 2.7 TB/s (BLOCK_M=32) while nvjet already runs o at 3.2-4.2 TB/s, and there is no reduce node or gap to drop here. Upside is bounded at ~+0.9% geomean even on full success; the realistic prize is the m=1 row alone (nvjet's m=1 kernel takes 5.0 us for the same 16.8 MB it moves in 4.0 us at m=8).

### Justification
Mechanism: o at m<=32 is a 16.8 MB bf16 stream (n=8192, k=1024), floor 3.5-3.6 us; master runs one non-splitK nvjet_sm90_tst_64x{8,16,32}_64x16_2x1 kernel at 5.0 us (m=1), 4.0 us (m=8), 4.4-4.6 us (m=16), 4.7-5.2 us (m=32) inside reported medians of 6.4-7.0 us (BEST-lineage profile 9aa98e889be140eb and the parent run 9f0ad00bbc874d31 agree). So nvjet is at 3.4 / 4.2 / 3.7-3.8 / 3.2-3.6 TB/s and the ~2 us reported-minus-kernel gap is per-node overhead a Triton launch pays too (the pooler Triton path showed ~0.3 us gap, so maybe 0.5-1 us of it is recoverable, which the hypothesis itself does not claim). Because the cuBLAS path is already a single launch, a gain can only come from the Triton kernel's own us being below nvjet's. The only measurements of this exact kernel family in the workspace are the parent's: 20.2-20.5 us for 67.1 MB at BLOCK_M=16 (3.3 TB/s) and 25.2 us at BLOCK_M=32 (2.7 TB/s); scaled to 16.8 MB that is 5.1 us at m<=16 and 6.2 us at m=32, i.e. flat at m=1 (5.1 vs 5.0), a ~1.1 us loss at m=8 (5.1 vs 4.0), a ~0.6 us loss at m=16 and a ~1.0-1.5 us loss at m=32. The hypothesis's 'target ~4 us kernel' needs 4.2 TB/s, which no Triton kernel here has reached, and its verify_1 row is mis-stated: it says '6.9 us, same' but the m=8 nvjet kernel is 4.0 us (4.2 TB/s), the hardest row in the layer, listed as 'faster'. What is genuinely different from the parent: each program's stream is 128 KB and 4 stages x (64x256x2 B) = 128 KB of weight smem, so the whole 16.8 MB is issued in the prologue (effectively every byte in flight at once, far more memory-level parallelism than the pooler's 64 KB per SM), which could lift bandwidth; against that, the kernel is 4 K-steps long so prologue/epilogue, launch ramp and the one-wave tail are a large fraction of a ~4-5 us kernel, which usually hurts short kernels relative to cuBLAS's tuned 2x1 cluster kernel. Shape arithmetic vs TASK.md: the four target shapes weigh 62 and o is ~11% of decode_1/16/32 and ~9% of verify_1 (6.6-7.0 us of ~58-75 us per load); a full success (say -1 us on all four rows) is ~+1.5% on four loads, ~+1.0% geomean, inside the judge's +-1.5% CI, and the realistic m=1-only success is ~+0.3% geomean. Per-shape err_ns is ~20 ns on 6.8 us so the rows are readable, but note o rows swung 6.52 -> 6.89 us (decode_1), 6.62 -> 6.77 (decode_32) and 7.58 -> 7.32 (verify_16) between two runs with identical o code, so run-to-run noise on this layer is +-0.2-0.4 us; a 0.4-0.6 us move at m=16 is not distinguishable from noise, a 1 us move is. verify_16 (m=128) and verify_32 (m=256) are correct flat controls (verify_32 should be added to the expected list); the m=33 test stays on cuBLAS by the m<=32 guard. Weakest link: the bandwidth premise. The claim that the Triton stream beats nvjet here rests on no measurement, the family's two data points say the opposite, and o is the layer where nvjet is closest to the floor (m=8 at 4.2 TB/s), so this is the member of the family with the smallest absolute prize (<=1 us per row) and the highest bar.

### Interference and A/B needs
No companion. A per-shape gain is unambiguous: on o m<=32 the graph contains one Triton launch plus a fixed-shape torch.empty, nothing landed reaches that path, and the harness compares against the parent's own rows. A per-shape loss is interpretable from the profile without another run: the Triton kernel's own us gives 16.8 MB / t; if it lands near the family's predicted 5.1 us (BLOCK_M=16) / 6.2 us (BLOCK_M=32) the loss is the known plain-tl.load streaming ceiling, if it is well above that the loss is short-kernel fixed overhead (4 K-steps, prologue-dominated), and reported-minus-kernel gives the launch/gap cost on the Triton path versus the ~2 us on cuBLAS.
