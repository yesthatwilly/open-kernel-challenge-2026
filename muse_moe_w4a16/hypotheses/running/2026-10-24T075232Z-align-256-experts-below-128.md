# align-256-experts-below-128

## Change

In kernel_fn, for num_tokens < 128 only (bs=1, 8, 16, 32 and the bs=4/33 tests), call moe_align_block_size(local_ids, block_size_m, N_EXPERTS) exactly as baseline.py does (256 experts, the expert_map-mapped ids with -1 for remote experts unchanged), instead of master's moe_align_block_size(local_ids, block_size_m, bank['count']) (32). bs=128/256 keep master's 32-expert call as the in-run control. Nothing else changes: same block_size_m pick, same Marlin calls, silu_and_mul, post-norm. Why: master is 3.2 us slower than SGLang at bs=1 (80.2 vs 77.0 us, err 0.48 us, ~4%) and 1.5-2.6 us slower at bs=8/16/32, while it sits at parity at bs=128/256; the only GPU-side difference from baseline.py on master is the 32-expert align, so it is the prime suspect for the small-M deficit. Supporting datapoint: align-single-token-ep (neutral by geomean) replaced exactly this align at bs=1 and its decode_1 shape went to 0.999x (76.99 vs 76.91 us) against master's 0.960x, a per-shape move well outside the 0.5 us error that the geomean verdict hid. A plausible mechanism (unverified; no sgl_kernel source here) is that the align kernel's handling of -1 ids depends on num_experts (e.g. a signed index into a 32-entry shared counter array lands remote routes in a local bucket, adding a phantom m-block and one extra expert's weight pass to both Marlin GEMMs), so the attempt file should record, from the profile, the align kernel name and the Marlin time at bs=1 under each setting.

## Expected

- `bs=1 *` faster: restores baseline.py align at bs=1, where master is 3.2 us (4%) behind SGLang; align-single-token-ep already showed decode_1 at 0.999x when this align was swapped out
- `bs=8 *` faster: same change; master 0.9 us behind (0.6%), likely inside noise
- `bs=16 *` faster: same change; master 1.6 us behind (0.9%), likely inside noise
- `bs=32 *` faster: same change; master 2.6 us behind (1.2%)
- `bs=128 *` flat: control: master 32-expert align kept
- `bs=256 *` flat: control: master 32-expert align kept

## Risk

This partially reverts the parity hypothesis's one structural change, on the shapes where it measured below SGLang; it can only bring those shapes to parity (identical to baseline.py there), so its value is a clean >=1.0 base to stack post-expert-norm-triton-small-m and block-size work on, plus the answer to why bs=1 lost 3.2 us. If decode_1 does not move, the 3.2 us is not the align and the next suspect is the Marlin call itself (block_experts length) or run drift. Low risk: the 256-expert call is what the judge's SGLang column runs every time on the same inputs.

## Adversarial review

Reviewed 2026-10-24T092246Z by an adversarial subagent (model opus).

**Verdict: proceed.** Justified, but the change works for a different reason than the one it gives. Master's profile shows that at every M<128 shape the 32-expert call takes moe_align_block_size_small_batch_expert_kernel (4.4-6.7 us), while at bs=128/256 (numel>=1024) it takes the regular moe_align_block_size_kernel (2.6-2.8 us). That split matches where master lags SGLang and where it sits at parity, and passing 256 experts routes M<128 onto the regular path that SGLang uses. One run is readable from the align kernel's own profiled time; no companion is needed.

### Justification
Mechanism: plausible and better supported than the hypothesis itself claims. Evidence from archive/sglang-marlin-path-parity/fb2cc736613747df.profile.txt: master runs moe_align_block_size_small_batch_expert_kernel at bs=1 (4.4 us), bs=8 (4.9), bs=16 (5.5) and bs=32 (6.7). At bs=128 and 256 it runs moe_align_block_size_kernel<int> (2.6 and 2.8 us). sgl-kernel picks the small-batch kernel when topk_ids.numel()<1024 and num_experts<=64 (from my knowledge of sgl-kernel; there is no source here to check). With 32 experts master meets both conditions exactly at M<128. SGLang, with 256 experts, never does. This explains the parity at bs=128/256 and the deficit below 128 without needing the speculative 'phantom m-block from -1 ids' story. align-single-token-ep fits it to the microsecond: at bs=1 its align_single_token_kernel took 1.2 us against the small-batch kernel's 4.4 us, a 3.2 us saving, which is the measured 80.23->76.99 us move. The phantom-block mechanism is contradicted, not supported. Marlin's profiled time at bs=1 went UP (41.1->45.5 us) in that run while the shape got faster. Profiled Marlin time is too noisy to judge by: identical Marlin code moved 150.9->116.3 us at bs=16 and 611.8->677.9 us at bs=256 between those two runs. So the attempt file's plan to read 'Marlin time at bs=1 under each setting' cannot decide anything. Record the align kernel names and their GPU times per shape instead. Expected sizes: the saving is (small-batch time) - (regular-path time), roughly 2-4 us per shape. Relative to the shapes that is about 2-3% at bs=1, ~1.5-2% at bs=32 and ~1-2% at bs=8/16. Only bs=1 (and perhaps bs=32) clear the 1.5-2.5% per-shape drift noted in LESSONS. The expected directions (faster below 128, flat at 128/256) are consistent with TASK.md, all loads weighted equally (60), and these shapes being far above their floors (Marlin-dominated, 3-7x SoL). Weakest link: we do not know what the 256-expert regular path costs at small M. If it is moe_align_block_size_kernel plus a separate count_and_sort/fill kernel, it may cost ~3-4 us, not 2.5. Then bs=8/16/32 save almost nothing and bs=1 recovers less than align-single-token-ep's 1.2 us kernel already did. The headline is very likely to stay inside the CI. It must be judged per shape and by the align kernel's own time. Note also that the bs=128/256 'in-run control' is only a drift control: those shapes already use the regular kernel, so 32 vs 256 there would be flat either way.

### Confounders on the current kernel
- sglang-marlin-path-parity (landed): this hypothesis partially reverts that patch's single deliberate difference from baseline.py, the 32-expert align, on M<128 only. That makes it a sub-change A/B of the landed patch, not a confounded interaction. Everything else on the launch sequence (block_size_m pick from bank['count'], Marlin args, is_ep, silu_and_mul, post-norm) stays identical to baseline.py, so any movement is attributable to the align call. The change would behave the same if the landed patch were tuned differently, except for block_size_m, which baseline.py also picks from bank['count'].

### Interference and A/B needs
No companion warranted. (i) An ab-revert of sglang-marlin-path-parity would remove the whole Marlin pipeline and is meaningless. (ii) There is nothing in the landed patch to tune that interacts with the align-kernel choice. (iii) A control isolating a sub-change is impossible because the change has one sub-change. The run is readable in both directions without help. If the per-shape profile shows the small-batch kernel replaced by the regular path and its time falling, a gain is attributed. If the regular path's time at M<128 is no lower than 4.4-6.7 us, a flat or lower result is explained, and the headline need not be read at all. bs=128/256 serve as a free drift reference. Do not burn a replicate. Identical code is never re-run anyway, and the align kernel's own time is the reproducible signal.

### Open questions
- Record per shape, from the profile, every align-related kernel (name, calls, GPU time) under the 256-expert call. Does the regular path at M<128 launch one kernel or two (align plus count_and_sort/fill), and does it beat the small-batch kernel's 4.4-6.7 us?
- At bs=1, align-single-token-ep's 1.2 us kernel is likely already cheaper than the 256-expert regular path. If this run confirms the small-batch-kernel story, the natural stacked follow-up is single-token at M==1 plus this change for 2<=M<128. Another is calling moe_align_block_size with num_experts=65 (the smallest count >64): that avoids the small-batch kernel while keeping the cumsum and padding small, but needs a check that ids in [32,65) never occur (they cannot, given expert_map) and that block_experts and n_padded stay correct.
- Does the small-batch kernel's handling of -1 ids (signed index into its per-thread counter rows) add work beyond its own runtime? Profiled Marlin time is too noisy (±20-30% on identical code) to show it. align-remote-sentinel-ids (queued) is the better probe, and the two should not be run stacked on each other until this one is harvested.
