## What changed, and where
- `rewrite/lib/hotcold_moe.py` (moe-only; decode never includes it):
  - `_hm_experts(..., fork=None, keep=False)`. With a fork and a wgmma-hot H, e0 is recorded on the caller's stream
    after cache3 is allocated and poisoned. The fork stream waits on e0 and runs the hot half (wg gather, w13, w2), then
    records e1. The cold half (Engine A K1 + K2) stays on the caller's stream. The caller's stream waits on e1 before

    `state['hm']['fon'][T]`, which prepare sets.
  - The fork stream and both events are made in prepare (`_hmf_prepare`), once per device. They are held at module
    level (`_HMF_FK`), not in the state, so the judge's state copy never has to copy a Stream. They carry no data.
  - The race (`_hm_gate`) takes names. The serial `H<h>` candidates are joined by `H-16f` and `H-8f` (H-16f is also
    the T=256 winner's forked twin) whenever moe_fork_canary passed for that T and code. Everything else is the same
    race: kernel_fn graphs, read flush, the same p10 / p50 / p90 draws and 2 rounds, `_rt_select`'s worst-draw rule,
    and the same margin (1.03, or 1.02 with a tight IQR).
  - **moe_fork_canary** (`_hmf_canary`) is named and synced, and runs per T and per code BEFORE any forked timing. It
    uses 2 inputs (the judge-style p90 draw and skewed-4hot, both with hot experts). It checks the forked block against
    its serial twin, with cache3 poisoned: out and cache3's local route rows bitwise, eager and over 4 alternating graph
    replays. Before each replay both graphs' outputs and the hot half's scratch (wg_xs, wg_hs) are filled with junk.
    Engine A's and wg's error words must be unchanged. Inputs and graphs stay alive until after the last replay. A
    code that fails does not race.
  - Fork gate (audit fix): the race ranks every candidate against master, so on its own a fork could displace its
    serial twin on a better worst draw by any amount. A forked winner must therefore also beat the serial race's pick

## Risks / what could fail
- The judge's eager or graph runs on its own stream: the fork waits on e0 recorded on that stream, and the join puts the
  caller's stream behind e1. A missing edge would race the post-norm against the hot w2. The poisoned replays in
  moe_fork_canary are there to catch that, but a rare race could still slip past 2 inputs x 5 comparisons.
- The judge's state copies share the module-level stream and events. That is safe for sequential calls and graph
  replays. Two copies running concurrently on different streams would share e0 / e1, but the judge has not been seen
  to do that.
- Adding 2 graphs per draw changes the race's interleave slightly, so the serial ratios could shift a little against
  9f8a518e.
- H-16 at T=128 is a near tie. If H-16f only just clears 1.02-1.03 vs master, a judged gain may sit inside the noise
  (±1.2% on identical bytes). At T=256 the fork must also clear 1.01 over H-16 in every round, which a ~2.5% block
  gain (W6-D's x0.975 at T=256) may not reach; then T=256 stays serial H-16 as on the board. Judge the M=128 / M=256 rows against their own SGLang time.
- Only wgmma-hot codes can fork: a Marlin-hot H's cache3 aliases cache1. If a Marlin-hot H ever won T=256, it would have
  no forked twin.
- Kill: no forked candidate wins at either T (Q1), or a forked route's judged rows do not beat 9f8a518e / cf937dd3 on
  their own reference.
- Expected: T=128 routes H-16f if the fork's ~7% holds in moe (M=128 row about -10..-20 us vs master's ~272 us);
  T=256 H-16f at x1.02-1.05 over H-16 (about -8..-15 us).
