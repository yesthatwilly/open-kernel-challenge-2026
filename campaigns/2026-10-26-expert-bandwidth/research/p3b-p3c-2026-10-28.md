# P3b + P3c: Engine A per PC on the H100 (2026-10-28)

Written 2026-10-28 ~07:10Z (date -u). Read-only analysis of two ncu captures on gpunode06 (H100 SXM, 132 SMs,
ncu 2026.2.1, `--clock-control none`, cache flush per pass). No ssh, no GPU job, no okc. The only files written are
this report and `research/p3bc_analysis.py`. Not committed here; the main thread commits musespark.

**Inputs.**
- P3b: `takemyjob/prototypes/hopper-probe/runs/20261027-232220-gpunode06/out/nodejudge-p3b/`. This is production v4 at
  T=8 / T=16 and v1 at T=1 on the pre-S3 snapshot 498afbd8. GPU 1. 36 launches, 37 replay passes each.
- P3c: `runs/20261027-232407-gpunode06/out/nodejudge-p3c/`. This is the current board's T=1 path: Engine A v6 K1
  `ea6_kernel<1,3,2>`, K2 `ea6_kernel<0,2,2>` and `ea_combine_kernel`, on d01f5b75 (master 81c9802e). Decode 1 at
  k_local 1 (reps 0, 1, 4, 5) and k_local 4 (reps 2, 3, 6, 7). GPU 3. 24 launches.
- P3, for pairing: `runs/20261027-203904-gpunode06/out/nodejudge-p3/prof/ncu.csv`.
- The pre-registered rules are `### p3b` and `### p3c` in `takemyjob/.../queue-staged/registrations.md`.

**Tags.**
- **[M]** a counter, a per-PC value or a source/SASS line.
- **[D]** arithmetic on measured values.
- **[E]** an estimate, from a stated model.
- **[I]** inference that no counter confirms.

**Method.** `python3 -I research/p3bc_analysis.py <outdir>` (stdlib only).
- It runs `research/p3_audit.py` unchanged on P3 and on P3b. That gives steps, records per SM, P = distinct_local,
  I_step and tensor-busy exactly as the P3 audit normalized them.
- It parses the three CSVs of each capture.
- It classifies every PC into a named region and writes `p3bc.json` and `p3bc_tables.md` to `<outdir>`.
- The source page is SASS only, with no line table. PCs are therefore classified by SASS pattern and position, using
  the per-kernel offset maps in `REGION_MAPS`. Each map was read off the listing. The script checks:
  - every executed or sampled PC falls in a named region;
  - each consume body holds 32 LDSM and 32 (one-tile) or 64 (two-tile) HMMA;
  - the SASS is identical across every launch of a kernel.
- The CUDA sources (`.worktrees/eb-carrier/rewrite/lib/cuda/ea*_kernels.cuh`, `ea_common.cuh`, `ea_combine.cuh`)
  appear verbatim in both snapshots, so source and SASS were matched one to one.
- Per-PC "Instructions Executed" comes from the SASS-patching pass. Its mbarrier spin loops run slower and iterate
  more, so the instrumented total is 1.03-1.12x the hardware total. All per-step instruction figures below are hardware
  equivalents:
  - every region except the try_wait spin loops is deterministic, so its instrumented count equals the hardware count;
  - spin = `smsp__inst_executed.sum` minus the deterministic regions.
- "Per step" means per 256 weights (one m16n8k16).
- "Record" means 36,864 B: 65,536 weights, 32 steps per consumer warp.

Per kernel, a + b k from the k=1 / k=4 pair, standalone under ncu [D]:
- K1: 7.96 + 6.81 k us;
- K2: 7.49 + 3.58 k us;
- combine: 4.61 + 0.09 k us;

M09 measured the same path in production as 12.5 + 10.2 k us (graph-timed).
- The per-expert slopes agree within 3%: streaming is the same with or without the profiler.
- The fixed part is 7.6 us larger standalone.

ncu serializes the kernels and flushes caches before each pass, so P3c lacks two production effects:
- the PDL overlap: K2 is launched early and co-resident, its prologue and weight prefetch run under K1, and the
  combine's launch overlaps K2;
- warm L2: x, topk_ids and h come from L2 in production.

What P3c sizes is each kernel's own fixed work. It does not size the production hand-off [I].
- `griddepcontrol.wait` costs <= 40 cycles per warp in K2 (`ACQBULK` at 0x02fe0 for the consumers, 0x01ed0 for the
  epilogue warp, 0x06650 for the producer), because K1 has already completed.
- K1 skips it altogether (flags & 4 = 0: K1 is the graph's first node).

- K2's h wait is the biggest single non-loop item in P3c [M].
- Its cause [I, from the source and timing]: the producer issues the CTA's first two 36,864-B weight records before
  `griddepcontrol.wait`, and h's eight 1-KB copies after it. Under ncu that is 9.5 MB across 132 SMs, about 3.2-3.5 us
  at the observed ~2.7-3 TB/s, before h lands.
- In production those weight copies start while K1 is still running (PDL, K2 co-resident). The exposed part there is
  K1's completion, the PDL release and h's copy from L2, which P3c cannot see.

**Expected effects, checked.**
- K1's SM-active time "dominated by the ramp" [D]: at k=1 the ramp is 7.9 k cycles, 38% of a consumer warp
  (prologue 3.2 k + x staging 2.2 k + first-record fill 2.5 k). The steady-state body is 8.5 k (41%). So the two are
  about equal, not ramp-dominated.
- K2's long_scoreboard "on griddepcontrol.wait and the K1->K2 hand-off mbarrier, not the consumer loop" [M]: yes for
  the hand-off mbarrier (bfull, 100% long_scoreboard). The griddepcontrol.wait part is not observable under ncu.
- The consumer loop's share at k=4 [M]: body 86.5% of K1's hardware instructions (21.59 / 24.97 per step), and 95% with
  the per-record control. This meets >= 80%.

### 3.3 The combine (`eac::ea_combine_kernel`, T=1: grid 1, 256 threads, 187 registers)

- Standalone [M]: 4.71 us (k=1) and 4.98 us (k=4).
  - Of that, 2.0 us has no SM active (launch and teardown under ncu).
  - It runs 2.66 / 2.97 us (4,835 / 5,401 cycles) on its single SM.
  - Instructions: 705 / 858 per warp across 8 warps.
- Issue profile [M]:
  - issue active 29-32% per SMSP;
  - 2.0 warps active and 0.45-0.48 eligible per scheduler.
- Stall per issue [M]:
  - long_scoreboard 1.72-1.81;
  - no_instruction 0.86-1.00 and imc_miss 0.64-0.95;
  - wait 0.71-0.79;
  - not_selected 0.51-0.54;
  - short_scoreboard 0.46-0.58;
  - math 0.16-0.22.
- Of ~6.9 warp-cycles per issue [D]:
  - 26% is global-load latency: w, ids, tw and y rows, all DRAM misses under cache flush;
  - 28% is instruction and constant fetch for a 1,072-PC kernel that runs once on cold caches;
  - 10% is fixed-latency dependence;
  - 14% is issue itself.
- So it is a latency-bound single-CTA chain, as registered [M/D].
- PC sampling gives 2-3 samples per launch on one SM, too few for a per-PC statement [M].
- In production y and ids are L2-hot. M09 put the combine on the critical path at 2.5-3.8 us per call. That is
  consistent with the 2.7-3.0 us of SM time here plus a shorter boundary [I].

### 3.4 The fixed-cost levers, ranked

Each lever is sized from P3c's standalone components [M/D], with the production estimate [E] anchored on M09:
- a = 12.5 us;
- k=0 floor 7.0 us (three launches, prologues and an empty combine);
- ~5.5 us from the floor to a (fill and hand-off);
- combine 2.5-3.8 us.

| rank | lever | what it removes (P3c, standalone) | production estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Persistent T<=8 kernel** (K1 + K2 + combine in one launch; grid barrier for h and for the row norms) | 2 of 3 boundaries (1.9-2.1 us each, no SM active); 2 of 3 prologues (1.6-1.8 us each: entry fetch 0.70-0.76, build_map barrier 0.56-0.80, split 0.23-0.34); the combine's cold fetch (28% of its stall time); the h hand-off becomes an in-kernel barrier + L2 read | **3-4.5 us** of a [E]. Capped by the 7.0-us k=0 floor, which is mostly boundaries and prologues; it subsumes rank 2 and most of 4 |
| 2 | **Fused K2 + combine epilogue** (last-arriving CTA per token, or all K2 CTAs after a per-token counter, doing the row norm and weighted sum) | the combine kernel: 2.05 us boundary + 2.66-2.97 us one-SM latency chain; the y round trip | **1.5-3 us** of the 2.5-3.8 us critical path [E]. The cheapest to build; grid-parallel beats last-CTA |
| 3 | **K1 x staging before build_map** (x does not depend on routing; issue its 8 KB right after the barrier inits) | K1's 1.23-us bfull wait, hidden behind the 1.8-us prologue | **<= 1.2 us** standalone [D]; less if x is L2-hot in production [E] |

M09's decision stands (a fixed-cost T<=8 path is the T=1 lever). P3c ranks the build order:
- the persistent kernel if the plan can take it;
- otherwise the fused K2 + combine epilogue first;
- plus the x-staging reorder, which is a few lines.

### 4.2 What a fragment-order repack removes

The repack puts in one lane-local word the eight codes of one m16n8k16 step, and serves the row scales by quad
broadcast.

- **ext_prop: 0 at today's pair encoding, <= 2.0 per step at best.**
  - Today a 4-pair word costs 1 + 2 + 2 + 6 = 11 ops. Each step uses 2 pairs from each of 2 words (qg row g, qu row
    g+8), so 11 per step on average [M].
  - A fragment-order word still holds 4 pairs and still yields 4 bf16x2 per step. At the same pair encoding it is the
    same 11 per step [D].
  - The only reducible part is pair 3, at 6 ops (2 `SHF.R` + 1 `IMAD.SHL` + 3 `LOP3`). The floor for any 4-pair 32-bit
    word is 9: dq3's exhaustive search (FINDINGS.md "dq3"), so <= 2.0 per step.
  - dq3 already delivered that cut with all-ALU rotations (4 `SHF.L.W` + 5 `LOP3`): +1 ALU and -3 FMA-heavy per word.
    VERDICT-dq measured x1.000-1.019 and it was killed (LOG 10-27 00:35Z).
  - Since the ALU is the busiest pipe (2.4), the repack gains on ext_prop only if its encoding reaches 9 ops without
    adding ALU ops: for example, keep the IMAD.SHL left shifts on the FMA pipe [I].
- **scale_prop_rt: 0.**
  - Each step needs {S_g, S_g} and {S_g8, S_g8}: two distinct scales, each a `PRMT` (byte to both halves) + a `LEA`
    (x16 + bias), so 4 per step whatever the storage [D].
  - Quad-broadcast storage changes only the load: the 4 scale `LDS.128` per record could ride in the code loads,
    -0.125 per step at most.
- **mul_bf16x2 4, HMMA 1, LDSM 1: unchanged.** About 0.03 per step of address math goes with the second code stream.
- **Net: <= 2.1 of 21.6 per step (<= 10% of the body, <= 8% of I_step)** [D]. Only the pair-3 encoding carries it.

### 4.3 What m-tile reuse removes (r = 2 / 4: one B fragment feeds r m-tiles; K_s = 512 / r keeps the record bytes)

- `LDSM` goes from 1 to 1/r per step: **-0.5 (r=2), -0.75 (r=4)** [D].
- Shared wavefronts per step go from 3.25 to 2.25 / 1.75. HMMA, the per-weight dequant (19 per step) and the
  per-record control are unchanged.
- That is 2.3-3.5% of the body's instructions. It relieves an LSU that runs at 9-12% of peak with 0 excess wavefronts
  and mio_throttle <= 0.036 (P3) [M]. So it attacks no measured limiter [I]. This matches the k-slice consumer's
  timing kill (LOG 10-27 00:35Z: "B-fragment loads are not the consumer's limiter").
- The register cost of r=2 (~111-119 against the 112 cap at 2 CTAs/SM, P3 audit section 5) is the binding constraint,
  not the instruction count [D].

### 4.4 What is left, for scale

At the dq3 floor and r = 4 the body would be about 9 + 4 + 4 + 1 + 0.375 + 0.25 + 0.2 ≈ 18.8 per step (-13%) [D].
Of that, 4 HMUL2 + 4 scale ops + 9 ext ops = 17 per step are per-weight dequant that neither lever touches.

Two larger instruction levers are therefore outside these two experiments [I]:
- the per-record control (1.6-2.1 per step, which a bigger record per barrier wait would amortize);
- the 4 scale ops per step, which a precomputed bf16x2 scale would remove at +33% bytes (excluded by the 0.5625 B per
  weight budget).
