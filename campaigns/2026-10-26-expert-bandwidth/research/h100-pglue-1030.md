## Question and decision rule (pre-registered by the caller)

How much of prefill's expert block is non-GEMM glue per class (turn_1 T=753, turn_4 T=3012, first_turn T=16384)? The
glue covered here is router/top-k, the local-id remap, align2 count + place, norm-gather, and the post-expert norm
weighted sum. Is there a legal cut worth >= 1% of the prefill headline? If so, it becomes a prefill check candidate;
otherwise this note records the anatomy for the plan. Headline elasticities come from `hyp.py triage
muse_prefill_layer`: t1s 21.9%, t1f 11.4%, t4s 20.6%, t4f 12.7%, fts 22.6%, ft full 2.7 / 3.6 / 4.4%.

## Method

- **Kernels.** `_local_expert_ids_kernel`, `_align2_count_kernel` / `_align2_place_kernel` and `_norm_gather_kernel`
  are verbatim copies from master. Chunk 512 / 512 / 1024 and dist fill match the judge log 9f934846:16-19. The
  norm_gather config was swept over nw {4, 8} x {1, 2} x the resident-CTA grid, as prepare does.
- **SGLang's `_post_expert_norm_weighted_sum_kernel` is not on pypi.** `ws` is the nodejudge shim with remote-slot
  loads masked, and the shim is NOT SGLang's kernel (see below).
- **Judge-shape inputs.** x is [T, 4096] bf16. Routing is random top-8 of 256, which gives about T local routes: 752,
  3056 and 16319, with n_padded 1024, 3328 and 16560. A `skewed` stress routing (5x local routes) was also run.
- **Timing.** CUPTI device kernel durations from torch.profiler, which is the same "GPU time" the judge profile
  reports. Each variant ran 7 shuffled interleaved rounds x 20 launches, in two modes:
  - cold: a 256 MB flush before each launch;
  - warm: an untimed launch right before, so inputs are L2-resident when they fit. This is the in-layer condition:
    the producer has just written them.
- **Exactness.** ng2 / ngb vs master ng: bit-exact at nw 4, at most 1 ulp at nw 8/16 (nw changes master's own tree
  as well); tail untouched. wsR vs the shim: bit-exact at equal nw. All ws variants vs an fp64 reference: 100% within
  1 ulp, apart from cancelling sums.

## The cuts tried

1. **ng2 / ngb: norm_gather with R independent row loads in flight.** The hypothesis was that the persistent slot
   loop serialises be -> sid -> row latencies. **Refuted.** The ratio vs master's best is 1.01x at T=753, 0.93x at
   T=3012 and 1.03x at T=16384. Master is already at the HBM copy floor at T >= 3012. At T=753 the 1.6 us gap to the
   floor is launch and tail latency, about 0.06% of the headline.
2. **wsR: post-expert wsum, one program per token, a scalar `if id >= 0` around each slot.** This means no load, no
   reduction and no barrier for remote slots. It is bit-exact against the shim at equal nw: 2.6x faster than the shim
   at T=753 and T=3012, and 2.7x at T=16384. It runs 1.13x above the copy floor at T=16384.
   - **It is not a lever against SGLang.** The shim is not SGLang's kernel. On the judge H200, prepare's graph gate
     (3fb4e723) measured a nearly identical kernel, the persistent-grid "(False, 8)" with scalar skip, at **1.14x
     slower** than SGLang at T=753 (14.1 vs 12.4), 1.007x at T=3012 and 1.039x at T=16384. So SGLang's kernel is
     already at the wsR / copy-floor level.
   - Non-persistent wsR could still beat SGLang by about 3 us at T=753. The H100 measures 5.25 against the judge
     profile's 8.4, but that comparison crosses devices and was never measured on the judge. The value is at most
     **0.12% of the headline**.

## Verdict (per the pre-registered rule)

**There is no legal glue cut worth >= 1% of the prefill headline, so this lane has no prefill check candidate.**

- If every main-stream glue kernel cost zero, the headline would gain **2.6%**: turn_1 1.04%, turn_4 0.79%,
  first_turn 0.81%. What can actually be recovered is only the floor gaps. If all of them closed at once, that is
  about **0.7%**:
  - t1: align2 latency, about 5 us;
  - t1: wsum, about 3 us;
  - t1: norm_gather, 1.6 us;
  - t1: lid, 1.2 us;
  - t4: about 7 us;
  - first_turn: the H200 HBM gap of norm_gather and wsum, about 60 us. This is not measurable on the H100.
- The largest single piece is the norm_gather pass. Its best structural cut is to fold it into rega w13:
  - A = gathered x rows times (1 + w), loaded by row pointer, since TMA cannot gather on sm_90.
  - The per-row rstd factors out of the GEMM: sum x^2 along K in the mainloop and scale the accumulator rows by rstd
    in the epilogue.
  - The xs write and read go away. The ceiling is **0.93%** if w13 pays nothing for the gathered A path. That is
    still below the bar, it is a rega mainloop rewrite, and it is not bit-exact: A is rounded before the rstd scale.
- The post-expert wsum cannot fuse into w2. The row RMS spans all 32 N-tiles of a slot, so it needs a second pass
  over y either way.

## For the plan

- Expert glue is 2.8-3.5% of the sliding rows. Every HBM-heavy piece of it already sits at or near its copy floor
  (norm_gather, SGLang's wsum). What is left is latency at T=753: align2 place 7.9 us, lid 1.2 us. Do not mint glue
  fusions unless they stack: their combined realistic value is <= 0.7%.
- If a rega w13 rewrite happens for other reasons, fold the pre-expert norm + gather into it (ceiling 0.93%), with the
  rstd-in-epilogue form above.
- A cheap stack-only item: align2 can take raw topk_ids with local_start read on the device. It already writes the
  local ids, so the lid launch goes away (~1.2 us per shape, ~0.05%).
