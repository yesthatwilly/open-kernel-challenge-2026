## 0. Pre-registration (written 18:50Z, before any hh2 timing run)

### 0.1 The cut the bar needs (step 1)

Decode headline = geomean over 6 loads; `hyp.py triage muse_decode_layer` (board 1.6904, 7cc034f9) gives each row's
share = its elasticity. The hot half (wg w13 + w2) sits in four rows: verify 32 sliding (427.7 us, share 10.6%) and full
(700.4, 6.0%) at T=256; verify 16 sliding (321.1, 11.1%) and full (460.2, 5.5%) at T=128. For a judge-us cut D256 / D128
of the row:

    d ln(headline) = D256 x (0.106 / 427.7 + 0.060 / 700.4) + D128 x (0.111 / 321.1 + 0.055 / 460.2)
                   = D256 x 3.335e-4 + D128 x 4.652e-4        (D in judge us)

Hot half alone on the judge (1749Z anatomy, w13 + w2 without gather): ~131 us at T=256, ~71 us at T=128. If a stack
cuts a fraction f of the hot half's SM time and the fork is zero-sum (h100-expert-1028), D256 = 131 f, D128 = 71 f:

    d ln(headline) = f x (0.0437 + 0.0330) = 0.0767 f

- **+2.5% headline (ln 1.025 = 0.0247): f = 0.32, i.e. the hot pair at x0.68**, a judge cut of ~42 us per T=256 row and
  ~23 us per T=128 row. A3 alone (x0.914 / x0.896) gives f ~0.095: +0.73%.
- **+1.2%: f = 0.156 (pair x0.84)**; ~20 us / ~11 us per row.
- moe (only verify 32 forks; row 291.1 us, share 16.7%): +2.5% needs ~43 us off the T=256 row (hot pair ~110 us on the
  judge: x0.61); +1.2% needs ~21 us (x0.81).
- In the production fork the end-to-end is what counts: a cut that only shortens SM-idle time the cold half already
  fills is worth less than its isolated ratio; a cut of the uncovered hot tail at the end is worth its full us.

### 0.3 Measurement and decision

- Routings as hh: dec-T256-{a,b,c}, dec-T128-{a,b,c} (decode anatomy), jd-T256 / jd-T128 (moe's judge draw).
- Sets: w13, w2, pair (w13 -> PDL w2), and **fork**: the production shape, hot branch (gather -> w13 -> PDL w2) on a fork
  stream next to a cold-half stand-in on the origin stream, joined in one graph. Every set: escal's `rt_time` (read
  flush before every replay, interleaved, rotated, empty graph subtracted), 61 reps x 5 rounds per process, GPUs 1 and 3
  as two replicates (10 rounds per cell).
- **Cold stand-in** (no ea4x source in the probe): k_cold, 264 CTAs x 288 threads, 112 KB dynamic smem (2 CTAs/SM, and
  no 187 KB wg CTA can co-reside with one: the residency arithmetic of the real ea4x, 113 KB), w13 then w2 with PDL,
  each CTA spinning to a clock64 target with the ncu per-SM spread (0.89-1.14 of mean). Its length is calibrated per
  routing so that cold alone = R x the dq0 hot pair alone, R from the judge anatomy (decode 7cc034f9 `[hotcold]`:
  T=256 136.0 / 134.5 = 1.011, T=128 146.6 / 74.4 = 1.97; moe T=256 H100 halves 223.5 / 117 = 1.91), cycles split
  w13 : w2 = 0.64 : 0.36 (ncu ea4x per-SM averages). A `bw` arm (the -a routings) also streams the judge's cold bytes x
  0.687 (H100/H200 HBM) so the cold half loads HBM as on the judge. The spin arm is the [cyc] model of SM residency
  (halves never share an SM); the bw arm is its HBM sensitivity.
- Harness validity (pre-registered): dq0 fork / (cold alone + dq0 pair) should land near the judge's fork-vs-serial
  gate (x0.97-0.99, decode `[w6m-fork]`); if it is < 0.90 the stand-in overlaps too well and fork ratios are flagged.
- **Decision rule (from the brief):** take the forked end-to-end cut of the best stack vs dq0 at decode-like T=256 and
  T=128 (mean over a/b/c), convert to judge us by the hot-pair ratio judge/H100 (131 / 128.7 at T=256, 71 / 67.4 at
  T=128: consumer-bound, cycles transfer) and into headline by 0.1:
  - **>= +2.5% -> 10-30 phase-A build of the stack** (parts and expected per-row us);
  - **+1.2-2.5% -> build A3 + the best single part as one judge check;**
  - **< +1.2% -> A3 rides the next decode/moe candidate only.**
- Each part is kept only if bit-exact (w1 / nx) or, for the split, max diff <= 1 bf16 ulp on < 1% of outputs.

## 1. Verdict (rule 0.3)

**Band 1.2-2.5%: build A3 + the stack's best form as one decode judge check; it does not reach the 2.5% bar.**

- Best forked stack, **wh** = A3 + exact N + an R=4 per-k16 A ring with `wait<2>` for N >= 48 (a3's stage loop for
  N16 / N32): decode-like forked end-to-end **x0.892-0.918 at T=256, x0.945-0.955 at T=128**, all bit-exact.
  - That is -24.6 judge us per T=256 row and -10.4 per T=128 row, i.e. **decode headline about +1.31%** (1.690 -> ~1.712).
    w4 (the ring at every N) gives +1.32%, the same within noise.
  - Against A3 alone (+0.74% by the same arithmetic), the extra parts add about +0.57%.
  - The 2.5% bar needs a forked cut of ~32% of the hot half. The stack reaches ~19% at T=256 and ~14% at T=128 [cyc].
- **moe: A3 only.** On moe's judge draw (jd-T256: 8 hot experts, 6 of them N32 chunks) the parts add nothing. Forked,
  every variant reads -14.5..-15.8 us (A3 -15.8), about +0.9% moe headline. The ring is neutral to negative at N32.
- **The judge check (decode).** Port to decode's wg text (:15330) and moe's (:1128), routed by N:
  - A3 (bank permutation in prepare).
  - nx: 8 tensor maps (box rows 16 x nbk), expect_tx = 9216 + 2 x 16 x nbk x 128, bucket = nbk - 1, plus the
    Wg<48/80/96/112> shapes.
  - The ring K-loop for NB >= 48.
  - Expected per row (judge us, estimates from H100 cycles): verify 32 sliding 427.7 -> ~403, full 700.4 -> ~676;
    verify 16 sliding 321.1 -> ~311, full 460.2 -> ~450.
  - Expected headline 1.690 -> ~1.71.
  - Kill: below A3 alone (verify 32 sliding > ~416). On moe ship A3 alone.

## 3. Parts that lost (quick runs 20261029-114641 / -115109, 11 reps x 2 rounds; directions far outside noise)

- **(a) Split-K x2 for w2 (`as`, `wns`): KILL.**
  - It is slower: pair dec-T256-a x0.975 vs a3's x0.922; wns x0.897 vs wn x0.820.
  - Even where the host rule picks no split (SPL = 1), its code path costs 4-7% (T=128 w2 x1.07 vs x0.89).
  - The fp32 partial round trip (64 KB per N128 half-item) and the doubled per-item fixed costs eat the
    1.45 -> 2.91-wave gain.
  - Not exact: 3479 of 1.58 M w2 outputs differ (0.22%). Max bf16-ordinal distance is 29229, which is sign flips of
    near-zero sums.
  - The w2 tail stays a lever only for a design without a global partial round trip.
- **Dynamic item scheduling (`wnd`, `w4d`; atomic counter, item ids through smem): KILL.**
  - Isolated: pair x0.895 vs wn's x0.820 (T256-a), x1.008 vs x0.923 (T128-a).
  - Forked: x0.937 vs x0.896. It was meant to help hot CTAs that start staggered behind the cold half; it does not.
  - Cause not isolated. The K-step table shows wnd +80-120 cyc/stage at every N, so it is a per-stage cost, not the
    item order. The consumers' extra full-barrier wait per item and the sentinel do not explain that much. Left open.
- **Ring at N32:** neutral to slightly worse (w13 w1 822 vs a3 796 cyc/stage). That is why wh keeps a3's loop there.

- **The ring reaches ~1.1x the floor at N >= 112.** It cuts N128 from 1296 to ~1115-1170.
- **At N <= 64 every variant is stuck at ~760-950 cyc/stage**, 1.8-3x the floor. That is ~100 cycles per k16 per

  ring's ARRIVE / commit / DEPBAR) and its dependency chain remain.
- **What is left is issue / latency of the dequant at small N.** More consumer warps per SMSP or fewer dequant
  instructions would cut it; that is the rega2 lane's question, not more MMA overlap.
- **nx's 8 instantiations cost ~2-6% at an unchanged N** (N64: 993 vs 970; N32: 845 vs 796; same NB, same loop):
  code size or allocation. It pays only where padding drops a chunk below N128 (N80 1063 vs 1294).

## 5. What transfers to the H200 judge

- **Transfers [cyc]:**
  - The K-step table and every isolated ratio (consumer-bound, same SM).
  - The SASS facts: 168 regs, 0 spill, no C75xx, DEPBAR.LE 0x1 / 0x2 in the ring.
  - The fork's residency model: 2 x 112 KB cold CTAs exclude a 187 / 204 KB hot CTA on both GPUs.
  - The losers' directions.
- **Does not transfer [hbm]:**
  - Absolute us. The calibration (cold alone = R x the dq0 pair) is in node us.
  - The bw arm's byte rate.
  - On the H200 the ring is less binding, so ratios may improve slightly. The stand-in's R comes from judge anatomy,
    so the fork ratios are a judge-shaped model, not a judge measurement.
- **Not covered:**
  - The real ea4x kernels. The stand-in has their grid, smem and per-SM spread, but no ALU or L2 contention with the
    hot CTAs.
  - The judge's exact per-expert decode counts. The a/b/c spread (+-2 us forked) brackets the shape.
- **The judge check is the measurement.**
