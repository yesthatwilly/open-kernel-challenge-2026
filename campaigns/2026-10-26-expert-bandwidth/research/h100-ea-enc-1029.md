## Question (pre-registered 2026-10-29T19:45Z, before any run)

Are Engine A's moe kernels consumer(dequant)-bound anywhere, and do A4b / A5b pay there?

- Kernels (the board's judge routes, 8418677e profile): T=1 `ea6_kernel` K1 (w13) / K2 (w2, v6pe2 two-wave grid 264);
  T=8 (verify_1), T=16, T=32 (decode rows), T=128 (verify_16) and the T=256 cold half (verify_32): `ea4x_w13_kernel<2,false>`
  / `ea4x_w2_kernel<2,false>` (v4xs at T <= 16, v4cxs above; consume = one-tile `eak::consume`, consume2 for 9..16-route
  passes).
- **Arms** (one compile, bitwise-checked against B every draw): **B** = board dq2 (ext_prop codes + L3/L4 paired scale
  PRMT + IMAD); **E4** = A4b codes (9 ops/word vs 11; ALU 7 + FMA 2 vs 8 + 3 by count) + B's scales; **E5** = A4b codes +
  A5b packed scales (one LOP3 per step, + PRMT on odd steps: 1.5 ALU / step vs B's 1 ALU + 1 IMAD).
- **Modes:** normal (TMA from HBM after a 256 MB read flush) and **consume-only** (past the first ring fill the producer
  re-arms each slot with a plain arrive: no TMA, consumers re-read resident smem; HBM out of the loop = the consumer
  rate, as h100-t1-1029 Q4 did for ea6).
- **Timing:** `_rt_time`, 10 interleaved rounds x 11 order-rotated reps per draw, empty-graph baseline subtracted; arms
  compared as per-round ratios (median [min, max]). ea4x at `_ea4x_draws` (p10/p50/p90 by distinct local experts for
  T <= 32, judge routings at 128/256); ea6 at forced n = 2/3/4 local experts, per-record rate from the n2 -> n4 slope.
- **ncu** (`ee-ncub`, master's board at all six judge shapes; `ee-ncu`, every bench arm and mode): issue active, ALU /
  FMA / tensor pipe, stall reasons, DRAM throughput.

**A priori expectation (written before the run).** Engine A's consume loop is ALU-pipe-bound (L4, which moved 0.9
ALU op per step to the FMA pipe, gained x1.066 in eac). E4 removes 1 ALU + 1 FMA op per k16 step (of ~19.7 issued, ALU
9.56): expect +5-10% consume rate. E5 trades the IMAD back for ALU work (+0.5 ALU, -1 FMA per step): expect it to be
neutral or worse than E4 in Engine A, unlike in the wgmma rega tile.

**Classification rules** (per kernel, per T):
- `consumer-bound on the H200` if the consume-only time, scaled to the judge clock (x node MHz / 1980), is >= 0.9 x the
  H200 streaming time of its bytes at a practical 4.3 TB/s (90% of 4.8) - i.e. HBM cannot be what limits it there;
- `HBM-bound on the H200` if that ratio is <= 0.75; between: `mixed` (both floors within 25%; the judge pays a coupling
  loss on top, as at T=1 in h100-t1-1029 Q4).
- On the node itself: HBM-bound when normal-mode DRAM >= 80% of peak or normal / consume-only >= 1.25.

**Decision rule (from the brief):** a kernel whose consume-only rate improves >= 8% (E4 or E5 vs B, median of rounds,
both replicates) AND that is consumer-bound on the H200 -> a moe check candidate (headline estimated with
`hyp.py triage muse_moe_w4a16`); HBM-bound on the H200 everywhere -> close cheaper-dequant for Engine A.

## Result 1: SASS of the port (CPU build on gpunode06; `probes/eaencs/results/`)

Microkernels hold exactly one `consume` / `consume2` call (32 k16 steps), so per-step = count / 32 and arm differences
are exact. Pipe classes by opcode (ALU = LOP3/SHF/PRMT/IADD3/LEA/ISETP/SEL/MOV...; FMA = IMAD*/FFMA/HMUL2...). cu13.0 and
cu13.3 give the same counts (ea4x DQ3 +8 instr total on cu13.3).

Readings `[cyc]`:
- **E4 removes exactly 1 ALU + 1 FMA (IMAD.SHL) op per step**, as counted a priori. **E5 removes 2.5 issued per step**:
  -1 ALU -2 FMA from E4's codes plus the IMAD, +0.5 ALU for the odd-step PRMT, net ALU -0.5 vs B.
- **My a-priori worry about E5 was wrong.** After L4, Engine A's step is issue-bound, not ALU-bound: B issues 21.5 per
  step against 18.8 ALU-pipe cycles (9.41 x 2). E5's ALU is 17.8 cycles against 19.0 issued, so it stays issue-bound
  and the extra PRMT is free. E5 >= E4 by static count everywhere.
- Both arms also free 10-15 registers per thread (ea6 79 -> 64), with 0 spills, so they are admissible everywhere.
- **Calibration against measured history** (same SASS lens): L3 removed 2.0 ALU instructions per step and read x1.097-1.119
  in the eac consumer-only bench, then +5-7% per expert row on the judge. L4 (0 issued, -0.9 ALU) read x1.066 / x1.015
  and +3.2% on the headline. E4 has -2.0 issued and E5 -2.5, so the expected consumer-only gain is **~x1.08-1.12 (E4),
  ~x1.10-1.13 (E5)**. That is a projection, not a measurement.

## Verdict (per the pre-registered rule)

- **The measurement leg is not met: GPU blocked.** No consume-only ratio was measured: GPU 0 and GPU 2 hit the 85 C
  guard, and GPU 7 was refused. So the rule's ">= 8% measured" condition can be neither met nor failed. Cheaper dequant
  for Engine A stays **OPEN**. It is not closed, because the close branch requires HBM-bound on the H200 everywhere,
  and the existing evidence says the reverse.
- **Provisional (static + history):** E4 -9.3% and E5 -11.6% issued per step, issue-bound, no spills, bit-exactness
  proven on the CPU. Both projected around the 8% bar (x1.08-1.13). The kernels are at the consumer-bound edge on the
  H200. If the consume-only run confirms >= 8%, **E5 (A4b + A5b) is the moe check candidate**, on all Engine A kernels at
  once: ea6 T=1 and ea4x T=8..256. It is one bank recode (`EAENC_PERM` + the A5b scale repack, prepare-time,
  lossless, needs dq2's existing [0x08, 0x7E] check) plus `DQ 4` instances.
- **Headline estimate (triage).** Each row is 1/6 of the geomean. Engine A kernels are ~94-97% of every row except
  verify_32 (~55%, cold half). decode_1 is capped by its HBM floor at <= ~2.5-4 us (t1 Q4). L3/L4's transfer (judge
  gain ~0.5-0.8 x the bench gain) puts it at **~+2-4% headline**, central ~+3%. That straddles the 2.5% value bar. A
  judge check is the authority, since the coupling loss is H200-specific.
