## Method

- **Codes = the judge's.** `reference.generate_weights(5151)` (master's reference.py, the judge's seed and generator, torch
  2.14.1+cu130 on the H100) through master's `_b12_encode` (AST-extracted from master submission.py, sha256 a24234053010df1c,
  not copied by hand) at R=32 (pooler) and R=16 (qkv_gate). x from `reference.generate_input(m, layer, 1000+m)`.
  The C++ side re-decodes every tile through the probe's layout and checks bit-exactness against the weight bits.
  - pooler: base 54, **max 51 per CTA** (judge log 51), mean 35.6 (judge ~36.6), 4559 escapes (judge 4687).
  - qkv_gate: base 54, **max 30 per CTA** (judge log 30), mean 18.5 (judge ~17.3), 2661 escapes (judge 2491).
  The worst CTA, which sets the gather tail, matches the judge exactly. The totals differ by about 3-7%, so the weights
  the judge's log (7e8be36a) saw are not bit-identical to these. Cause not chased; it does not affect the per-CTA maximum.
- **Incumbent = master's.** `_bf16_swapab_kernel` with `SWAPAB16_CFG` (pooler grid (64,2) SPLIT_K 2 BLOCK_K 256 stages 4;
  qkv (36,4) SPLIT_K 4 BLOCK_K 128 stages 4; BLOCK_T 16, its own split-K workspace and tickets), launched as
  `_swapab16_forward` does. It runs in a Python process (Triton), and the b12 arms run in the C++ process. Both use the
  same method: 256 MB read flush before every launch, an event pair, the empty-kernel baseline subtracted, rotating
  interleave, 9 rounds x 41 reps, and per-round medians. cuBLAS bf16 runs in both processes as the cross-process anchor
  (C++/py 1.008-1.033). The incumbent was timed before and after the b12 run, with a drift of 0.2-1%.
- **Arms:** GA0 bridge `br` (a7da6fa9 rotated b12x, the judged code), `br_g1` / `br_g2` (batched gather, at the end /
  overlapped), `uc1` (TMA boxes, no cluster, S6 2/SM at m8, S4 at m16) `_g1` / `_g2`, `uc1_s8*` (S8 1/SM), `b12r`
  (x resident, 419d2459 inst 18), cuBLAS, stream floor (12-bit bytes).
- **Per-SM floor (`--grid-div D`):** the same kernels run on only the first N/D rows' CTAs (128 -> 64 -> 32 CTAs), so each
  SM runs the same pipeline with 1/D of the HBM demand. A time that does not move with D is not HBM-bound.

## Verdict (pre-registered rule)

**Spend the bf16 judge check. Pooler m8 projects 0.939 (<= 0.95) and qkv m8 projects 0.906 (<= 0.95), so both are
routable. Pooler m16 projects 1.03 (optimistic 0.93) and should run as a diag only, or behind the gate.** The arm that
routes is `uc1_g1`: TMA boxes, no cluster, plain local mbarrier arrives, batched escape gather (GA1) after the stream.
No bridge variant projects under 1.09 at any cell. The bridge is per-SM-bound, so the gather fix cannot rescue it on the
judge.

Caveats:
- Pooler m8 sits only 1.1% under the 0.95 routing line, and the gate also requires every round <= 0.97. Expect a route
  there with p ~0.5. qkv m8 has a 4.4% margin.
- The projection is a two-point model. It reproduces the judged bridge within 0.2% and the b12r gate within about 2%,
  but uc1 itself has never been judged in a non-MEMBAR state.
- `uc1_g2` (the overlapped gather) loses to `g1` at every cell here, because the early gather stalls the stream.
  Route `g1` at m8. At m16, `uc1_s8_g1` beats `uc1_s8_g2` by 1 us on the node.

What the judge build should carry, in line with the adversarial review: a GA0 bridge diag arm (non-routable), `uc1_g1`
at pooler m8 and qkv m8 as routable cells under `_b12_max_ratio` = 0.95 with the read-flush `_cell_gate`, and
`uc1_s8_g1` at pooler m16 as a diag (or behind the same gate). Print GA0 - GA1, which should read ~3.9 us at pooler m8
and ~0.9-1.9 us at qkv m8. Expected headline if both m8 cells route: about +0.5-0.8%, inside the ±1.24% CI. Read the
result off the [b12m] ROUTE lines and the rows.
