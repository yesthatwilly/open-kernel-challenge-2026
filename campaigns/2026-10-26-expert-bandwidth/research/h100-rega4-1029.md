## Question (pre-registered)

With A5b's lower register/issue load, does more consumer TLP at the LOW class cut the w13+w2 pair >= 5% vs A5b+D+SC?
Decision rule: >= 5% pair cut at low (bit-exact, no spill in the k loop) -> prefill check candidate; else record the
low-class bound.

The register arithmetic fixes the shape: 2 x 384 threads at 80 regs = 61440; producer 80 -> 32 frees 6144 per CTA, exactly
consumers 80 -> 104. 104 regs holds N64 (32 acc + 32 A) but not N128 (64 acc), hence the chunk cap; smem
(2 x (dyn + 14 KB static + 1 KB) <= 228 KB) allows 3 stages, not 4.

Variant (a) (3 consumer WGs splitting the slab's N) was not built: at low 28 of 32 chunks are N16/N32 (pop 16/12/2/2,
random 2/30/0/0), so a third WG either idles (N16) or re-dequantizes the same 64 weight rows (a third more of the bound's
own work); an M=192 tile does not divide the 128-row record/tile layout. 2 CTAs/SM doubles consumer warps with no
duplicated dequant, so it is the clean test of "more TLP".

## Verdict (per the rule)

**Yes at low: SC-2CTA is bit-exact, spill-free, and cuts the pair 6.2-11.3% vs A5b+D+SC (low l2 0.896, hbm 0.938, random
0.887) -> prefill check candidate, low class only.** It must be dispatched by class: at high/ft it is 1.13-1.24x, so the
production form is "SC-2CTA when T is below a threshold, A5b+D+SC above it". The crossover lies between T 753 and T 3012
and was not measured. Sizing the headline needs the low-class share of the judge's prefill time; with the A3/A5b
node->judge transfer (held within 0.01-0.02) the kernel-level cut should carry.

Next steps, cheapest first: (1) a T sweep (e.g. 1024/1536/2048/2560) of arms 1 vs 2 to set the dispatch threshold;
(2) the master port: a second `rega_kernel` instantiation (STAGES 3, slab 64, `% 4` chunking, launch bounds (384, 2),
setmaxnreg 32/104, grid 2 x SMs, its own smem size) behind a T switch; (3) a node-judge run of that build before a
check. Remaining low-class losses: the +6.5% items from the chunk cap and the tail (SM active / elapsed 87%).
