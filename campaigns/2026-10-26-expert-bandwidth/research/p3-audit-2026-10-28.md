### 2.2 What the table says

**T=8, v4 w13: 3,981 cycles per record.**
- 487 are SMs idle while others finish [M].
- Of the 3,494 active cycles, about 720 are per-launch work spread over ~29 records per SM [D]. The fit gives
  19.5-22k SM cycles per launch for w13 [D, weakened]. That work includes:
  - the fix-up fences (~300 per record [M]);
  - build_map's and the fix-up's barriers;
  - ramp and tail.
- The remaining ~2,770 match the fitted steady state of 2,760-2,810 SM-active cycles per one-tile record [D, robust
  in the verifier's rerun].
- In steady state each SMSP issues about 1,608 warp instructions per record (I_step ~25.1) and issues nothing on
  ~1,150-1,200 cycles [D]. long_scoreboard dominates that warp time (~1,107-1,182 per record [D]).
- The busiest pipe, ALU, is busy 1,753 cycles: 50% of active and 63% of the steady state [M/D]. Nothing is
  saturated on average.

**T=16, v4 w13: 3,656 cycles per record.**
- The same steady state; the per-launch part is spread over more records (~48 per SM, ~430 per record) [D].
- Two of the six reps include two-tile passes. A two-tile record costs 150-420 more cycles (x1.05-1.15); the data do
  not pin this down [D, corrected].

**w2 vs w13.**
- w2 costs 330-500 more SM-active cycles and 600-680 more elapsed cycles per record than w13 at the same shape [M],
  with the same floors (ALU 1,759-1,767) [M].
- w2 streams half the bytes per pass, so it has half the records per SM, and its per-launch costs (fences,
  barriers, ramp, tail) weigh about twice per record [D/I].
- At T=8 most w2 splits are aligned, so membar is 0 there; barrier and idle carry the difference instead [M].

**T=1, v1.**
- The per-SM floors are the same as v4's: ALU 1,730-1,746, LSU wavefronts 879-890, tensor 384, issue 1,611-1,631
  [M/D]. It is the same dequant per weight.
- v1 differs in two ways:
  - Only 2.0-2.3 warps are active per scheduler: 4 consumers + 1 producer per CTA, 2 CTAs/SM, shared memory binds
    [M].
  - Few records per SM: 8-19 (w13) and 4-10 (w2). Per-launch costs therefore dominate: SM idle is 1,052 / 1,479
    cycles per record, and on w13 the fence is 485 [M].
- The SMSP issues nothing on 58-63% of active cycles [M].

### 2.3 What carries to the H200 judge, and what does not

The H200 ladder numbers (target 2,190; consumer alone 2,421; combined 2,793 at T=8 and 3,277 at T=16; all at 1.98 GHz)
are a different device and harness. They are not mixed into the table above.

**Per-SM compute carries over** (same SASS):
- ~25 warp instructions per step (marginal);
- 1,720-1,810 ALU-busy cycles per record;
- an issue floor of 1,590-1,730;
- ~860-990 LSU wavefronts per record at q=1;
- 384 tensor-busy cycles at q=1;
- 91 registers at 2 CTAs/SM.

So even with no waiting at all, the consumer needs ~1,750 ALU-busy cycles per record. That is 71-75% of the H200
consumer-alone figure and 79-83% of the target [D]. Reaching the target with today's instruction stream would need
the ALU pipe busy at least ~80% and issue at least ~73% of cycles [D]. These are per-SM compute statements.

**Not carried over:**
- the size of the long_scoreboard wait;
- SM idle;
- DRAM coupling;
- the H100's issue efficiency;
- anything about how the producer and consumer slow each other.

On the H100 the coupled kernel waits on stage data for at least ~30% of consumer residency at 69-76% DRAM
utilization. The H200's faster memory changes that balance, so the coupled case has to be measured on the H200
(section 4).

The H200 ladder rungs were built cumulatively and are quoted inconsistently:
- In FINDINGS.md, 3.98 TB/s is "C-R2: ring + exact dequant".
- In SINGLE-PASS.md, the same 3.98 is "compute-only C-R4 at 1 CTA/SM (3.78 at 2/SM)".

So the existing numbers are not a matched set. Matched rungs need the following.

1. **One consumer, byte for byte.** All three rungs run the production `eak::consume` / `eak3::consume2` from the
   same bytes. Confirm with `cuobjdump -sass` that the consume region's SASS is identical across the three builds;
   only the barrier wait and the producer may differ.
2. **One geometry.** All three rungs keep:
   - 288 threads (8 consumers + 1 producer warp);
   - `__launch_bounds__(288, 2)`, 91-96 registers, 0 spills;
   - 113,008 B of dynamic shared memory: pad any rung that needs less, so occupancy stays 2 CTAs/SM and 18 warps;
   - grid 264, D = 2, 36,864-B stages.
3. **Consumer-only rung.**
   - Fill both ring stages once, so the records sit in shared memory, and pre-complete the full barriers.
   - Loop the unchanged per-stage code (slot and index math, `__syncwarp`, empty-barrier arrive) over the resident
     stages long enough for clock64 and ncu to resolve, at least ~100 us.
   - Write a checksum of the accumulators so nothing is dead code, and check it against the production kernel on the
     same records.
4. **Pipeline-only rung.**
   - The real producer: two bulk copies per stage plus the x rows, expect_tx, empty waits.
   - Consumers do only the full wait, `__syncwarp` and the empty arrive: no LDS, dequant or HMMA.
   - Stream the same experts as the production pass list for each T.
5. **Combined rung.** The production kernel, plus a variant with clock64 stamps around the two `mbar_wait`s per
   stage. Check that the stamps change nothing else in the loop's SASS. The stamps measure the consumer-wait /
   producer-wait split that P3 can only bound.
6. **Same draws and T.**
   - T=8: q=1, 8 routes per pass.
   - T=16: q=2 on 9-16-route passes.
   - Use the routing of the profiled reps (or one expert per pass at 8 and 16 routes) for all three rungs.
7. **Same measurement.**
   - Graph-timed, L2 flushed as on the judge, SM clock recorded (at least 1800 MHz on the H100 under R1).
   - The P3b metric set via ncu on every rung; cycles per record per SM at the measured clock.
   - Interference = combined minus max(consumer-only, pipeline-only).
8. **Where.**
   - The consumer-only rung's per-SM numbers transfer, so the H100 can measure it.
   - The pipeline-only and combined rungs are memory-bound and coupled, so their H200 values need the H200 or the
     judge (the H200 is held). On the H100 they give H100 values only.

## 5. The two independent next experiments

### Shared budget facts (measured, v4)

- 91 registers per thread (96 allocated).
- The register cap for 2 CTAs/SM at 288 threads is 112: 65,536 / (2 x 288) = 113.8, rounded down to a multiple of 8.
- `__launch_bounds__(288, 2)` turns any demand above 112 into spills. That is how the k-slice consumer failed (56-72 B
  of spills at T=16).
- Shared memory: 113,008 B dynamic. The cap at 2 CTAs/SM is 115,712 B (233,472 / 2 - 1,024).
- Both experiments must keep 0 spill instructions (`ptxas -v` and `sass__inst_executed_register_spilling`) and
  2 CTAs/SM resident, and must produce outputs bit-identical to today's kernel (same dequant, same HMMA order per
  accumulator).

### Experiment 1: m-tile reuse sweep, r = 1, 2, 4

One B fragment (x) feeds r m-tiles per warp. dq2 and each accumulator's k-order are unchanged.

**Design.** Hold the stage at 36,864 B, 8 consumer warps and D = 2, and set the k-chunk K_s = 512/r. Per-warp
staging 9·d·r·K_s then stays at 9,216 B. Only the x rows per stage shrink.

| r | K_s | weights staged per CTA (8 x 9·d·r·K_s) | x rows per stage | dynamic smem | fits 2 CTAs/SM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 512 | 73,728 B | 16,640 B | 113,008 B (measured) | yes |
| 2 | 256 | 73,728 B | 8,448 B | ~96,600 B | yes |
| 4 | 128 | 73,728 B | 4,352 B | ~88,400 B | yes |

With K_s = 512 at r = 2, staging would double to 147,456 B and the kernel would drop to 1 CTA/SM, which confounds
reuse with occupancy.

**Registers.**
- The task's model is 4rq + 2q + 4 + overhead. Calibrated on the measured 91 at r=1, q=2, the overhead is ~75. It
  gives:
  - r=2: ~99 (q=2), ~95 (q=1);
  - r=4: ~115 (q=2, over the cap), ~97 (q=1).
- The source carries two things that model leaves out. Both grow with r:
  - two k16-parity accumulator sets (`acc[tile][parity][4]`);
  - 12 code/scale registers per m-tile per k-quarter.
- Counting those: r=2 needs ~111 (a q=1-only build) to ~119 (q=2), and r=4 ~150-175 [E].
- So r=2 is at the edge of 112, and r=4 does not fit 8 consumer warps at 2 CTAs/SM.
- Run r=4 at 4 consumer warps (160 threads: cap 200 registers) together with its own r=1 / 4-warp control, or drop
  it.
- Gate every variant on 0 spills and record its register count.

**Predicted work [D].**

| | r=1 | r=2 | r=4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| LSU wavefronts per step at q=1 (1.25 + 2q/r) | 3.25 | 2.25 | 1.75 |
| LSU wavefronts per step at q=2 | 5.25 | 3.25 | 2.25 |
| LSU instructions per step | 1.5 | 1.0 | 0.75 |

Dequant ALU work per weight does not change.

**Readout.**
- Consumer-only cycles per record at T=8 and T=16 (section 4), plus the P3b counters per variant.
- Keep r > 1 only if cycles per record fall by at least 5% (the VERDICT-dq bar) with 0 spills and bit-exact outputs.
- P3 predicts a small gain at q=1: LSU work is 25-30% of active and mio_throttle is at most 0.036. The gain should be
  larger at q=2 and in the combined rung, where TMA fills share shared memory [E].

### Experiment 2: fragment-order repack, tested at r = 1 only

**Change.** A lossless permutation, done in prepare():
- One lane-local 32-bit word holds exactly the eight codes the lane needs for one m16n8k16 step: rows g and g+8,
  k = 2cq, 2cq+1, 2cq+8, 2cq+9. The A fragment then decodes from one word with no cross-word extraction.
- The two row scales for that k16 step are stored with the quad's words.

**Budget.**
- Bytes per weight must stay 0.5625, so the stage stays 36,864 B and shared memory stays 113,008 B.
- That holds only if each scale is stored once per row per 16 k and read by the quad's 4 lanes with a broadcast load.
  A per-lane scale copy would add 2 B per 8 weights: 0.75 B per weight, +33% bytes.
- Words per lane per step are unchanged (4 B of codes per step, as now), so live registers should stay at or below 91.

**Expected.**
- The ALU-class ops per step (source estimate ~11 ext_prop + 4 scale decode) fall toward ~10 [E].
- dq3's proven 9-op-per-word minimum applies to today's word layout, not to a fragment-order one. The new count is to
  be measured.

**Readout.**
- I_step and ALU busy per record from the P3b counters, and consumer-only cycles per record at T=1, 8 and 16.
- Bit-identical outputs.
- Adopt at a gain of at least 5% in cycles per record.

**Sequencing.** Test it separately from reuse. If both win, a combined run follows.

## 6. Disagreements between the lenses, and how this audit resolves them

- **P3's issue efficiency was used to plan the H200 target.** Not done here. Most of P3's unused issue is
  memory-coupled waiting, which does not transfer. Only per-SM floors are carried over (2.3).
- **"Cut ALU instructions" vs "break the warps' lockstep".** Both remain hypotheses.
  - The ALU floor (~1,750 cycles per record) is a per-SM fact and caps any fix of waiting alone at ~80% of the
    target's budget.
  - The lockstep is measured (the e^-E excess), but on the H100 it mixes with memory waiting.
  - The repack tests the first lever; the matched rungs measure the second on the H200.
- **math_pipe_throttle range.** Both lenses printed it as 1.00-1.10 on v4; the CSV gives 0.95-1.14 (used here).
- **The two-tile increments.** The instruction lens's and the issue lens's two-tile costs come from the same
  30-launch fit. They move 35% or more when verify 32 is dropped, so their agreement is not independent. Given here
  as undetermined (150-420 cycles per record).
- **"ALU constant across q".** Circular on the ALU-fit launches. Restated on routing-exact launches only.
- **The epilogue's `gs[le]` global load (:7487).** Both lenses missed it. It is a non-mbarrier long_scoreboard source
  in the loop and lowers the consumer-wait bound from at least 33% to at least ~30%.
- **ALU demand.** It sits anywhere from 9.7 to 13.7 per step depending on which opcode is double-charged. Plans here
  use busy cycles, which do not depend on that. The budget-closure check points to ~13.5. P3b's opcode mix settles
  it.
