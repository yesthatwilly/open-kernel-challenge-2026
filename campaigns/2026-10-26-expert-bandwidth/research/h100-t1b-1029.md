## What bounds T=1 now (answer)

- **Both floors still coincide on the judge, now lower.** The whole chain's consume floor is ~1.10 us/record/SM
  (K1c 1.07-1.08, K2c 1.03), and the measured ring floor is 1.113-1.121. The judge runs ~1.32 per record/SM (v6pe2
  slope 7.94 us/expert x 0.967), **~18-20% above both**. The structure is a coupling loss plus a serial tail: K2 can
  only consume after K1's last h, then cmb runs.
- **The node confirms the serial part.** With consume at 0.70x of the ring, the full chain still costs 12-13% more than
  ring-only (F/Fr), and ~2 us of that is the cmb.
- **The judge's E4 elasticity was ~0.6.** A consume speed-up of 5-6.5% surfaced as -3.3% on the block, so the judge's
  T=1 is consume-coupled, not HBM-bound.
- **Fixed costs.** Intercept 4.5-4.9 us. The cmb is ~2 us of it. Glue outside the expert kernels is ~0 (KF = F).

## Cut tried: K1 with 16 consumer warps (CW2), bit-exact

Two groups of 8 consumer warps take alternate records, with the same per-record math, so the output is bitwise equal.
576 threads, 71 registers, so K2 stays co-resident (<= 72).

- **The D3 ring needs a cross-group guard.** Records i and i-3 belong to different groups, so a group first waits on
  slot empty (i/3 - 1). Without the guard the mbarrier parities alias, and that caused the crash.
- **D2 version:**
  - consume-only x1.086;
  - normal mode x0.91 (K1) and x0.958 (F) on the node.
- **D3 version, 2 replicates:**
  - consume-only K1 slope 4.55 -> 4.12 (**x1.105 per record, `[cyc]`; judge-scaled 0.98 us/record**). The intercept
    rises +0.7 us, so K1c is x1.032-1.038 at n2-4.
  - Fc16 vs Fc is x1.04-1.044 at n2-4 but **x0.92 at n2**. Weighted, it is -0.13 / -0.22 us (slower).
  - Normal mode: F16 = F (x0.997 / 1.003), K1x16 x0.973.
- **Verdict:** no gain at the judge's n mix. The per-record gain is cancelled by the intercept (the first fill shared by
  two groups, plus the guard). **KILL.**
