- **T <= 32 has a fixed glue cost of ~6.4 us per row**: R (route + pre-expert norm + align) ~3.9-4.2 us plus combine ~2.3-2.7 us.
  The router GEMM is fused into the dense P kernel (`dl_pooler_router`, 28.7-35.5 us on the node; the judge's T=1 path
  is b12p). It is not separable glue. The FFN-boundary `norm_residual` (~5 us) sits behind the unpooler GEMM, so folding
  the combine into it is not possible at any T.
- **T >= 128 front (the * entries):** the router side chain is `zero` -> `_t128_hilo` -> `routing_kernel` -> `_hc_align`.
  It runs on the fork stream next to the pooler GEMM (nvjet, grid 128 CTAs). **The pooler occupies 128 of the 132 SMs,
  so every side kernel is starved.** hilo takes 40.5 us concurrent against 9.0 us alone, and routing takes 40.9 us
  concurrent against 4.3 us alone (measured in the rf arm). The fork buys almost nothing: from the front start to the
  start of gather is 61.3 us at T=256. Running the same kernels serially would take ~66 us.
- **Judge-scale ceilings.** These use node glue us over judge row us, weighted by hyp.py shares:
  - Every glue kernel at zero, excluding the router GEMM: x1.049. Route + align + gather alone: x1.032. Combine alone: x1.015.
  - The whole T>=128 front glue hidden under the pooler: x1.012. That is 17 us at T=256 and ~11 us at T=128, the bound
    if the pooler were the only thing on the path.
- The judge profile (e4233998) agrees on the T <= 32 glue kernels (R 4.1-4.5, cmb 2.9-3.0 us). It shows hilo at 8.0 and
  6.4 us, i.e. the unstarved times. Its overlap is only ~22 us at v32s, so on the judge the side chain also runs
  effectively serially.

- **prio:** the zero kernel drops from 4.3 to 1.2 us. hilo still runs to the pooler's end (~46 us), because priority
  orders block dispatch and does not free SMs the pooler already holds.
- **rf:** hilo alone takes 9.0 us. The routing kernel then starves under the pooler instead (40.9 us), so the critical
  path moves without getting shorter.
- No r2 replicates were run: both arms point the wrong way, and the mechanism is clear from the nsys timelines.

## For the 10-30 plan
- **Glue is not a decode lever on its own.** At T <= 32 every remaining fold is either refuted (CLAIMS 40, 43, 66; EPF and
  cmbf on moe) or a redundant-prologue variant worth <= ~2 us per row, which is <= ~0.4% headline.
- **The only sizeable glue is the T>=128 serial front: ~17 us at T=256 and ~11 us at T=128 above the pooler GEMM.**
  Hiding it needs SMs for the side chain while the pooler runs. Options are an SM carve-out of the pooler (cublasLt SM
  count target, or a 116-SM custom tile; it is bandwidth-bound at ~1.75 TB/s, so it may not lose much), or routing +
  align fused into one small kernel that fits in the 4 spare SMs. The ceiling is x1.012 judge-scale; a realistic capture
  is about half, ~x1.006. That only pays as a rider on a T>=128 stack. A multi-CTA align (moe glue lane) also serves it.
- Side observation (not glue): in the board's T=128/256 timelines the hot `wg_kernel` pair and the cold `ea4x` pair run
  serially on one stream (cold starts after hot ends: 240.9 vs 240.6 us). moe's hybrid forks the cold half off the
  critical path. Check whether decode's hot/cold fork is gated off on purpose; it is worth far more than any glue cut.
