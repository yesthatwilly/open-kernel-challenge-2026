## Q3 plan and pre-registered reading rules (05:47Z, before any run)

- **t32anat r1 / r2.** In-process; arms `jr` (judge routes forced: post `ea.on.9..16 = v4xs`, `ea.on.17..32 = v4cxs`)
  and `jinc` (the twins' incumbents v4 / v4c, which give the x change's share). Unforced routing (the reference's
  draws, judge-like), 200 reps. An nsys graph-node pass of 30 reps per shape over jr and jinc gives the per-kernel
  start/end, PDL wait, critical path and gaps.
- **t32ncu.** ncu on `jr` alone, kernels serialized, `--clock-control none`, 4 profiled reps per shape. It reports
  DRAM % and SM % of peak, per-SM active cycles min / avg / max (tail imbalance = max / avg), warp instructions per
  HMMA, and DRAM / L2 bytes.
- **Reading rules** (characterisation, so no pin; these say what each number will be called):
  - A kernel is **HBM-bound** if DRAM >= 80% of peak (elapsed); **per-SM-bound** if SM% is above DRAM% or DRAM < 60%.
  - **Tail imbalance** matters if per-SM max / avg >= 1.10, sized as (max - avg) / elapsed x kernel time [cyc].
  - An **uncovered gap** is critical-path time with no kernel running (launch gaps, a PDL wait whose predecessor has
    already finished). It is a lever if it is >= 2 us per row on the node [cyc-like].
  - **w2 hidden under w13:** the share of w2's duration that overlaps w13's (from nsys). If w2's critical-path share
    is >= 30% of the row, the w13 -> w2 hand-off is a lever.
  - The share of each stage = its mean critical-path us / span. Shares carry over to the judge only as structure; the
    node's absolute us do not.

**Reading (by the pre-registered rules).**
- **Neither expert kernel is HBM-bound as a whole** (DRAM 65-74% < 80%), and neither is per-SM-bound (SM 37-43% <
  DRAM). The structure is streaming at the wall per expert plus fixed time. At the node's median k, the intercept
  (24-37 us) is 18-20% of the row [cyc-like].
- **Per-SM tail imbalance is a lever at T=32** (max/avg 1.10-1.12) and borderline at T=16 (1.08-1.09) [cyc].
  - Sized as (max - avg) / elapsed x kernel us: T=16 w13 5.6 + w2 3.6 = **9.2 us**; T=32 w13 12.6 + w2 6.0 =
    **18.6 us** on the node.
  - On top of that, SMs sit idle 12-16% of the elapsed time on average (avg active / elapsed 0.84-0.88), which is ramp
    plus tail.
  - Scaled by share to the judge's rows (w13 66% x tail 6.9-10.2%, w2 31-32% x 7.7-8.8%): about **7.7 us at decode 16
    and 13 us at decode 32**. That is an upper bound for perfect per-SM balance, and it clears the >= 4 us row bar on
    both rows.
- **Uncovered gaps: 0** (no launch gaps; every kernel is on the critical path). Not a lever.
- **w13 -> w2 hand-off.** w2 is resident 24-35 us early (PDL), but its bytes stream only after w13 ends: the w2 tail
  grows with all of w2's bytes. Its critical-path share is 31-32%, which is >= 30%, so by the rule the hand-off is
  flagged.
  - On the node, though, both kernels stream at the HBM wall, so overlapping them is zero-sum here (the same lesson as
    Q1).
  - On the judge it can pay only if w13 runs below the wall. w13's kernel-average DRAM is 74% of peak, but its
    per-expert marginal is at the wall, so the gain is bounded by w13's ramp / tail windows. Not a primary lever.
- **Combine:** 2.6-2.9 us of path at both T. Fusing it into w2's epilogue saves <= ~2.9 us.
- **For the t32 panel, ranked:**

     how the v4 / v4c static split maps records to the 264 CTAs and whether the imbalance is work or DRAM-channel

  2. The fixed ramp in w13 (part of the 17-21 us intercept).
  3. Fusing the combine (<= 3 us).

  Stream overlap and prefetch are not levers on the node.

**Note for the panel (code read, not measured), 06:35Z.** ea4 / ea4x split the work stream-K over W = passes x 64
super-groups x 8 k-chunks (w13), ending in a last-arriver ticket fix-up per split super-group.
- At the node median k this is ~23 units per CTA at T=16 (12 x 512 / 264) and ~31 at T=32. The integer split alone
  gives +-1 unit, i.e. ~3-4% per CTA.
- v4c cost weighting is a no-op at these T: ~3 routes per expert, so every pass is one-tile.
- Hypothesis for the remaining ~5-8% of the per-SM max/avg spread: the fix-up epilogues land on the last arriver,
  plus DRAM-channel variance between SMs. A finer unit, or a balanced fix-up assignment, is what a balance design
  would test. The panel should measure per-CTA unit counts and fix-up counts (the dbg stamps) before building.
