### 2026-10-28T2240Z: T=256 anatomy and a fork bug (eb-t256anat r1/r2 -152240 / -152811; eb-x256arms r1 -152246; eb-t256fx r1/r2 -153108 / -153111)

**BUG: the W6-M fork collapses into a serial graph when its stream is a pooled one.** `_hmf_prepare` makes the fork
stream with `torch.cuda.Stream(device)`, which hands out one of torch's 32 pooled streams per priority in round-robin
order. When that stream is the capture stream, `with torch.cuda.stream(fk["s"])` launches the hot half on the
capture stream itself. The graph is then depth 7 / width 1 (one stream in nsys), against depth 5 / width 2 when the
fork works.
- On the node, 4f5f2072's own prepare race reads `H-16f/H-16 x0.99-1.00` in most processes since 10-28. That covers
  the iso board party in 4/4 of the v4x iso runs and t256anat, x256arms and the eb-x256 iso runs. Older bytes, and the
  1.8941 lineage in the same iso runs, read x1.07-1.11.
- Forcing H-16f gives that serial graph. The cap arm `h16fx` (`_HMF_FK` with a fresh `torch.cuda.Stream(priority=-1)`
  at capture) restores depth 5 / width 2 / 2 streams: h16fx vs the pooled h16f is x1.027 (r1) / x1.019 (r2).
- The judge's af154932 prepare race reads `H-16f/H-16 x1.031..x1.066`, so the judge probably did not collide today.
  But the collision depends on how many pooled streams were drawn before (bytes, races and deadlines), so a final
  rerun can collapse silently. Cheap fix: never let the fork stream equal the current stream (check at capture, or use
  a high-priority / dedicated stream). Decode's _hc fork has the same pattern.
