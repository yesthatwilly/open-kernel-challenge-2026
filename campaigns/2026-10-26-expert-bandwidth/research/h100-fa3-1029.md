## Question and decision rule (pre-registered by the caller)

What bounds FA3 on the full verify rows (v1 / v16 / v32), and is there >= 10% on the node? A legal knob >= 10% faster
on a row that is not HBM-bound: a judge check candidate. HBM-bound at >= 80% of peak: the row is at its floor.
Otherwise: name the dominant inefficiency and a design that attacks it.

## Setup

- Inputs: the problem's own `reference.generate_input` (batch B, ctx 80000, tp8, layer full, q_len 8, page 1, spread
  agentic for B > 1, dist coherent), so lengths, the scattered randperm page table and its width (80008 / 86008) are the
  judge's. Query: normalized FP8 query as master's pre-attention kernel writes it (built in torch, not timed).
- Master's call (muse_attention_decode submission.py:1912, muse_decode_layer submission.py:8555, identical): B = 1

  num_splits / pack_gqa at FA3's defaults; varlen cu_seqlens_q; causal.
- Timing: one CUDA graph per variant, a 256 MiB read before every replay (cold L2, as the judge), 7 interleaved rounds
  (order shuffled per round) x 20 replays; ratio = master / variant per round, median (rmin..rmax in the logs). Two

- No legal FA3 knob reaches 10% on any row. FA3 picks its split count dynamically (prepare kernel); a larger
  num_splits is a no-op (bitwise equal), a smaller one is slower **and less accurate** (next point). The LOG:2043 note
  "num_splits=1 saves ~2.2 us" does not hold on the full verify rows: ns=1 is 21x / 5.6x / 3.1x slower there.
- Balance is not the lever: with a perfectly balanced 132-piece partition FA3's own kernels drop only 2% (v32 323.9
  vs 330.7 us; v16 170.8 vs 173.3), and the extra call's prepare + launch eats it.
- **Precision (transfers exactly): FA3's FP8 error vs FP32 grows with keys per split** (P rounded to FP8 in the PV
  wgmma; the coherent values make it a bias, not noise). v1: 6.3e-3 at FA3's 57 splits, 1.5e-2 at 16, 3.8e-2 at 4,
  7.9e-2 at 1. Master at v16 (8 splits) 2.6e-2, at v32 (4 splits) 3.9e-2; each request run alone 4-7e-3; the balanced
  split engine at k=3 halves v16 / v32 error (1.1e-2 / 1.7e-2). Any rewrite with longer chunks per CTA gets less
  accurate; shorter chunks plus a combine buy accuracy.

## Verdict (per the rule)

1. **No knob >= 10%.** Best legal setting: dense q + precomputed metadata, x1.05-1.06 on FA3's v1 call (~1.5-2 us)
   and x1.006 on v16 / v32. Dense q alone (a call-site reshape, bitwise-identical output, no kernel) gives x1.03-1.04 at
   v1 and stacks with the metadata fold (CLAIMS:82): a shelf item for that fold's stack, not a check on its own.
2. **Not HBM-bound.** v16 / v32 main loop at 59.5-62.5% of HBM peak and 72-75% of the measured scattered-row gather
   ceiling (2.72-2.79 TB/s on the H100). v1 at 30% DRAM: fixed-cost-bound (prepare 4 + combine 8 of 32 us).
3. **Dominant inefficiency (v16 / v32): memory-level parallelism per SM in FA3's paged mainloop.** Page size 1 forces
   FA3's non-TMA cp.async gather: one persistent CTA per SM, 3 warpgroups, one producer; the page-index load ->
   address -> cp.async chain shows as long_scoreboard, the producer/consumer handoff as barrier stalls; issue 34%,
   ~24-32 KB-equivalent in flight. Tail imbalance (SM active 88-90% of elapsed) is worth only ~2%, the split combine
   ~2-4%. **v1: fixed costs**: prepare (grid 1, 4-5 us), the combine on 8 CTAs (8 us, 25% of the call), main at 30% DRAM.

### Design that attacks it (v16 / v32 full verify, and decode's same rows)

A persistent paged-gather attention kernel, one balanced wave: the concatenated (request, token) key range cut into
132 x k equal stream-K items (balance is cheap to get but only ~2%); per CTA a ring of >= 4 stages x 64 tokens x 256 B
(K+V paired 128-B rows) = **>= 64 KB in flight per SM**, the measured requirement for the 2.7+ TB/s plateau; the
stage's page indices fetched one stage ahead into smem, so no address waits on a dependent load; 128-B rows loaded
paired but computed per head (QK and PV per 64-B half: no zero-padded FLOPs); FP8 wgmma; in-kernel ticket combine, as
splitkv_attn_sk_kernel's (CLAIMS:41/54), so no combine launch and no prepare kernel. Bound on the H100: FA3 main
323.8 -> ~236 us at v32 (x1.37) and 172 -> ~123 us at v16 (x1.40). Earlier custom full-verify kernels lost (s7
~179 / 367 us, CLAIMS:63; FULLV TG, CLAIMS:80): the bar is that a mainloop-only DIAG must reach >= 2.6 TB/s at v32 on
the H100 before any routing work. Shorter items also make the result more accurate (above).

Judge-row arithmetic, if the H100 ratio holds on the H200 (FA3 main x1.37 / x1.40): verify_32 full row 299.8 -> ~224
us, verify_16 full 161.4 -> ~119 us; with weight 16 per load that is ~x1.25 / x1.23 on the verify_32 / verify_16
loads and ~+7% headline on attention, before the decode port (FA3 main is 152 / 288 us of decode's 467 / 701 us verify
16 / 32 full rows, LOG:1881). On the H200 the headroom is probably larger (FA3 gains only 1.13x there, the gather
ceiling should scale toward 4.8 TB/s) but that is **untested**: the H200 is held.

## What transfers to the H200

- **Transfers:** the knob ordering and ratios of fixed-cost changes (metadata, dense q: per-launch costs; a larger
  share of the faster H200 row); the num_splits / pack_gqa behaviour (same scheduler, same 132 SMs); every precision
  number; ncu launch shape, occupancy, stall mix (same kernel, same SM); the 1-CTA/SM bytes-in-flight requirement
  scales with bandwidth x latency (the H200 needs more, ~1.4x, for its plateau).
- **Does not transfer:** absolute times, TB/s and DRAM %; the gather ceiling itself (2.72-2.79 TB/s is the H100's;
  the H200's must be measured); HBM floors (v32 196 us H100 vs 137 us H200; TASK floor 126.8 us).
- v1's main kernel scales with bandwidth (x1.44 H100/H200), so its H100 time overstates the judge's; its fixed costs
  (prepare 4 us, combine 8 us on 8 CTAs) transfer.

## Caveats

- sgl_kernel is pypi 0.4.9; the judge image's sgl_kernel build is unknown. The judge's own FA3 main times (148.6 /
  285.9 us) agree with the H100 picture (per-SM-bound), and its prepare / split behaviour matches CLAIMS:82's bitwise
  metadata check.
- The precomputed-metadata variant needs the tile semaphore (its last element) zeroed every call (a memset node here;
  the fold writes it for free); without that a graph replays into a fault. The fold branch already does it.
- The split-engine's pseudo page table was built outside the graph (a real version writes it in the pre-attention
  kernel); it lost anyway.
