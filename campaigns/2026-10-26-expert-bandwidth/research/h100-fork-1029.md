**Q1, the untested fork arm** (04:58Z, before any Q1 run). Variant bytes `var-56962224dbe4` = master moe
(v4x256c, `4caf9e6d`) plus one knob, `HMF_COLD_HI` (default 0 = board behaviour, verified as the vF null). Bit 1
launches the forked T=256 cold ea4x w13, and bit 2 the cold ea4x w2, with `cudaLaunchAttributePriority` -1. The
attribute is captured into the graph's kernel node. The hot half stays on the guarded RT_FORK_PRIO 0 fork stream.
Every arm forces the judge's T=256 route: H-16f with the cold half on v4cxs (cold_bits 112). Arms: bF (board, forked),
bS (board, serial: the fork-gain reference), vF (variant, knob 0), vC (`HMF_COLD_HI` 3: cold w13 + w2 at -1), and
vW (`HMF_COLD_HI` 2: only the cold w2 at -1).
Decision: **GO for a judge check only if vC or vW vs bF is >= x1.005, with the 95% interval above 1.003, in all 4
runs** (submission order ab and ba x 2 replicates), and vF vs bF is null. Otherwise keep RT_FORK_PRIO 0 as it is.
Expected: vC / vW slower (x0.95-0.99).

**Q2, route stability** (05:18Z; the board's 18 prepares were launched at 05:10Z, and none of their results had been
read). The metric is flips per class over 15-20 fresh cold prepares (isolate, fresh_build, no tests). A flip is a
prepare whose route for a class differs from that class's modal route. Classes: Engine A x 8 / 16 / 32 / 128, the
T=256 cold half, and the T=256 hybrid/fork pick. Also reported: the sd / min / max of every race ratio (s and d
per class), per bytes.
Decision: **eb-v4x-routes is "more stable" only if its flips are <= the board's in every class and strictly lower in
class 8 or class 128, with no class above 2 flips per 16 prepares and no prepare error.** The node's modal picks are
not the judge's; only the flip counts and the spread are the evidence (`[cyc]`-like: the mechanism transfers, the
margins do not).

**Q3, decode fork guard** (05:18Z, before the build landed). Collision count: for every board prepare, a post-prepare
path line compares `state["side_stream"]` with the current stream and torch's default capture stream (which
nodejudge's capture uses). A clash means the T>=128 router side work is captured on the capture stream itself
(serial). The captured verify 32 graph's width is the second witness. For the guard build, count how often the guard
picks its alternate (a trip). A/B: forkguard vs board, isolated (one judge-shaped process each), verify 16 + verify
32, both orders x 2.
Decision: **land-worthy if no row regresses with a resolved interval (> 0.3%) in either order and the guard keeps a
distinct stream in 100% of prepares.** The value is robustness: a tie is enough if the board collides at all on the
node. A resolved verify 32 gain >= x1.005 in both orders would make it a judge check on its own.

At priority -1 the cold w2's PDL CTAs take every SM the cold w13 frees, from ~111-121 us on. The cold half ends early
(~225 us), but the hot half (wg w13 imbalance 1.43, per h100-expert-1028) then runs nearly alone at its serial length
to ~345-350 us. Overlap falls from 85 us to 15-23 us. In vC even the 3.5 us hot gather waits ~190 us for an SM. On the
board, the hot w13 takes the drained SMs at ~132 us and the hot w2 fills the long cold w2's tail. Priority 0 on both
branches is the better order, and it is already what RT_FORK_PRIO 0 gives. This is the mirror of the 10-28 prio -1
hot-fork result (the hot w2 jumped ahead, +20 us). Whichever half is promoted, the overlap is lost.

**Decision (pre-registered): KILL both arms; no judge check.** Every run is resolved slower (vC x0.962-0.965, vW
x0.953-0.957), and vF is null in all 4 (x0.998-1.000). Keep master moe as it is (RT_FORK_PRIO 0, no per-launch
priority). No further priority variants are worth node time: both single-branch promotions lose, and the board's
equal-priority interleave is the best measured.
