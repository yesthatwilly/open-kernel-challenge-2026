## Question and decision rule (pre-registered by the caller)

Can a mainloop with >= 64 KB in flight per SM reach the paged-gather ceiling at full verify (2.72 / 2.79 TB/s at
v16 / v32, research/h100-fa3-1029.md)? Rule: mainloop >= 2.6 TB/s at v32 (>= 0.93 of the ceiling) AND end-to-end
<= 0.85x master's call at v16 and v32 with tolerance held -> judge-check build; 2.3-2.6 TB/s -> report what limits it
(ncu) and the next step; < 2.3 -> the gather cannot be fed at that depth with this design.

## Verdict

**Band 2.3-2.6: the mainloop reaches 2.52-2.54 TB/s at v32 (0.90-0.91 of the ceiling), 2.38-2.40 at v16.** The
end-to-end half passes (0.82x master at v32, 0.83-0.84x at v16, tolerance held, 23x more accurate than FA3), but the
rule needs both, so this is **not yet a judge-check candidate by the rule**. The memory half of the question is
answered yes: the same kernel with the math switched off moves 2.80 / 2.76 TB/s (v32 / v16) = the ceiling. The
limit is the math, which on its own only runs at about the ceiling rate (2.82 TB/s-equivalent), so loads and math
cannot overlap up to it (see "What limits it").

## Kernel (v6, the reported config `g8`: NS = 3 stages x 2 CTAs/SM)

- Persistent grid 2 x 132, 256 threads (2 warpgroups = the 2 KV heads, each warp 16 query rows = 2 tokens x 8 heads).
  Stream-K: the concatenated (request, 64-key tile) list cut into 264 equal contiguous ranges; both KV heads per CTA
  (the paired 128-B K and V rows).
- Loads: cp.async.cg 16-B, 8 lanes per 128-B line, into an NS-stage ring of 16 KB stages (64 keys x K, V), chunks
  XOR-swizzled by key & 7; the tile's page indices land by cp.async one full ring ahead (no address waits on a
  dependent load); per-CTA per-tile table built in the prologue; in flight 2 stages x 16 KB x 2 CTAs = **64 KB/SM**
  (NS = 4: 96 KB, same speed). One `__syncthreads` per tile.
- Math: S = Q K^T with **wgmma m64n64k32 e4m3** (Q in registers, K straight from the ring: the XOR layout is the
  128B-swizzle K-major layout; head h's k-steps are descriptor offsets 64h + 32s); V widened to f16 into a
  double-buffered 128B-swizzle MN-major tile (fence.proxy.async + named barrier per warpgroup), overlapping the QK
  wgmma; online exp2 softmax: fp32 max/scale, **ex2.approx.f16x2** straight into the PV A-fragment layout, row sums
  on HADD2; O rescale skipped (warp-uniform) when no row max moved; O += P V with **wgmma m64n64k16 f16** (P from
  registers), left in flight across the next tile's barrier + QK.
- Partials (fp32 O, m, l) per (CTA, request) at contiguous slots; **combine: grid barrier (all 264 CTAs resident),
  then one warp per (request, output row)** over all CTAs; self-resetting counters (no memset node, no prepare).
- Correctness on every row and rep (judge's own `generate_input`, new K/V written at cache_loc as the pre-attention
  kernel does): the counters reset between launches (graph replay safe).

| v5 dynamic pair stealing | 290-330 | 238 | balanced ends, but slower (below) |
| v6 = v4 + grid-barrier combine | 259.4-260.2 | 233 | e2e +9 us over the mainloop (ticket combine was +47-84 us) |

- Ticket combine (the brief's design): the last CTA per request merges 9-19 partials of 32 KB; the partials are
  evicted from L2 by the KV stream, so it runs at ~1.5 us per partial (22-34 us per request at v16) on the
  kernel's critical path. Distributed after a grid barrier: 3-4 us.
- **Per-SM imbalance**: co-located CTAs are (i, i + 90). With equal static work the lower-id CTA on every SM ends ~20%
  earlier (v32: 217 vs 254 us; v16: 113 vs 135): warp-scheduler priority. Dynamic stealing between a CTA pair
  (atomics measured at 17 cycles per chunk switch, negligible) balances the ends but costs ~8% SM throughput when two
  co-located CTAs interleave one range (finer chunks worse); static ratio splits by arrival order fail (arrival order
  is not the priority order). Modelled gain from perfect balance: <= ~2%.

## What limits it (ncu, v6 v32, `--cache-control all`)

DRAM 75.8% of peak (FA3: 62.5%), L2 hit 6.3%, issue active 52%, warps active 23% (16 warps/SM at 128 regs),
48 warp-instructions per key (v1: 107). Stalls per issue: wait 1.38, not_selected 0.86, barrier 0.67, short
scoreboard 0.57, math-pipe throttle 0.56, long scoreboard 0.55, gmma 0.47, mio 0.41, dispatch 0.37: a latency-bound
per-tile chain (barrier -> QK wgmma -> V widen -> named barrier -> wait -> softmax -> PV) with only 4 warps per
scheduler. **The math alone runs at 233 us at v32 (2.82 TB/s-equivalent), the same as the gather ceiling**, so with
the load issue competing for the same issue slots and barriers the overlap tops out at ~0.91 of the ceiling.

**Next step** (to clear 2.6): the math must run >= ~1.3x faster than the ceiling rate. FA3-style warp specialization,
1 CTA/SM: a producer warpgroup issuing all cp.async (low setmaxnreg), two consumer warpgroups at setmaxnreg ~232
with intra-warpgroup overlap (QK of tile i+1 issued before softmax of tile i), mbarrier full/empty instead of the
per-tile `__syncthreads`. Smaller levers: the proxy fence on the K ring (~1.3%, if it can be shown unnecessary), a
priority-aware static split (<= 2%). v1 needs its own split/combine (two-level or fewer, longer partials).

## What transfers to the H200

- **Transfers:** the per-SM structure: instruction counts, registers/occupancy, stall mix, the emulated FP8 mma.sync
  (any sm_90a), the CTA placement/priority imbalance (same scheduler), the combine costs' shape, and **the math-only
  time (233 us v32, 119-121 us v16), which is per-SM-bound** (same GH100 SM count and clocks). FA3's main kernel is
  per-SM-bound too (322 us H100 vs 285.9 us judge H200). All precision numbers.
- **Does not transfer:** absolute TB/s, the loads-only times and the gather ceiling (the H200's ~1.43x HBM must be
  measured); DRAM %.
- Projection, untested (H200 held): the memory side gets ~1.4x faster but the math floor stays ~233 us at v32, so the
  gate kernel becomes math-bound at ~235-250 us vs the judge's FA3 main 285.9 us (~1.15-1.2x, below the H100's
  1.24x); end to end vs the judge's full verify_32 row (299.8 us incl. prepare/combine) ~0.82-0.87. Borderline; it
  needs an H200 measurement or the warp-specialized math before a judge check.
