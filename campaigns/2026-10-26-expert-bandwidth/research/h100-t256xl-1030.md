## Question and rule (pre-registered in the brief)

At decode's T=256 shape, does some split H plus a cold arm cut the T=256 expert block's consume-only time by >= 3%
(judge-scaled) vs the scored route (H-16, cold v4cxs), bit-exact, in all 3 replicates? If so, build the decode change
that routes it, with a gate change only if the variance data justify one. Also explain why decode's T=256 race fails
the 0.98 gate in one round while moe's passes.

"Judge-scaled": the node's 1.83 GHz scales to the judge's 1.98 GHz by x0.924 for both sides of a ratio, so a
consume-only ratio is its own judge-scaled ratio. Per replicate the statistic is the mean over its 6 draws of the
per-draw median paired ratio; qualify at <= 0.970 in each replicate.

## Method

Node-only patch (default routes = master's bytes):
- ea4x (every Engine A v4x twin, and the E / XL modules built from it): pdl bit 512 = consume-only (eaenc's form).
- wh hot half: V = 78 = wh5ds | 64, a consume-only producer (hhxl's co5 form).
- `_t256b` runs at the end of prepare. It covers T=256 only, 6 judge-shaped decode draws per replicate
  (`_ead_cases` + the real router, as `_hcx_inputs`).
- H in {-32, -24, -20, -16, -12, -8, -4} x cold arm in {B = v4cxs (scored), E4, E5, E4XL, E5XL}.
- Exactness: each arm vs B at the same H, plus each H vs H-16 B.
- One interleaved `_rt_time` per draw over 189 graphs, 8 rounds x 12 reps, every replay read-flushed.
- Graphs per (H, arm): block = `_hc_experts(part="all")` in modes n (normal), c (hot V78 + cold bit 512 =
  consume-only) and h (hot normal, cold consume-only); cold alone n / c. Per H: hot alone n / c.
- Decode T=256 is serial on the node and the judge (`fk` None): block ~ hot + cold + tail.

## Verdict

**QUALIFIES: (H-16, cold v4cxs + E4XL or E5XL).**
- Block consume-only cut, judge-scaled: E4XL 3.7 / 4.2 / 4.3%; E5XL 3.6 / 4.3 / 4.2%.
- Every round of all 3 replicates is <= 0.974, and the result is bit-exact.
- No other split qualifies.
- The arm is the same one decode's race already measures at T=256. The lost gain is the **gate**, so the decode change
  is a gate change.

Calibration: the judge read this exact configuration at block E4XL/B 0.976-0.978. The node's c reading is 0.957-0.963,
so the transfer is ~0.55-0.6x, the same as ea-enc2 -> moe/decode XL. Expected judge gain is ~2.2-2.4% of the T=256
expert block. At ~45% of the verify-32 layer, that is ~1% on the T=256 rows, worth ~+0.2-0.4% headline. That is a
rider, not a stand-alone check.

## Why decode's T=256 race fails 0.98 and moe's passes (judge records)

| race (judge) | arm | median | [min, max] | median-to-bar margin | rounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decode 9263781b T=256 (serial block) | E4XL / E5XL | .976 / .978 | [.972,.982] / [.968,.984] | .004 / .002 | 10 |
| decode beaed2ad T=256 | E4XL / E5XL | .978 / .977 | [.975,.981] / [.973,.982] | .002 / .003 | 10 |
| moe 73156455 T=256 (kernel_fn, hot forked) | E4XL / E5XL | .965 / .969 | [.963,.972] / [.964,.981] | .015 / .011 | 15 |
| moe 922ddf4c T=256 | E4XL / E5XL | .968 / .969 | [.965,.975] / [.964,.976] | .012 / .011 | 15 |
| moe cold iso (both) | E4XL / E5XL | .948-.949 / .944-.946 | [.940,.964] / [.936,.965] | .03 | 15 |

**The cause is dilution, not variance.**
- Per-round spread is the same on both problems:
  - decode XL ranges are .006-.016 (sd ~.0033 from range/3.08);
  - moe's are .009-.017 (sd ~.0035);
  - the node's c-mode sd is .003-.005.
- **Decode** times the whole serial block. The cold half is ~47% of it on the judge (beaed2ad anatomy: all 279.9,
  cold 131.8 us), so the cold half's ~5% (moe cold iso .948) reads as 1 - .47 x .052 = .976. That is the measured
  .976-.978.
- **moe** forks the hot half (H-16f), so the cold half sits on the critical path with weight ~.67, which gives .965.
- With median .977 and sd .0033, P(round > .98) ~ .18, so P(any of 10 rounds) ~ **.86**. Decode's T=256 XL fails its
  gate in most runs even though every round's E/m is below 1 (max .984).
- moe at .966 has P ~ 1e-3 over 15 rounds.
- A cold-iso gate on decode would sit at ~.95 (.03 margin, P(fail) negligible).
