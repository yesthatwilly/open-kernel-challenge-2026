# m256 lane: anatomy of moe verify 32 (bs 256) and its biggest structural lever, 2026-10-30 (analysis only; gpunode06 GPU 1 not used)

Lane: one analysis plus an H100 node lane on moe's biggest remaining triage row. The source is
`hyp.py triage muse_moe_w4a16` on board 2.0753 (scored 922ddf4c):
`verify 32  bs 256  share 16.7%  ours 269.7 us  sglang 629.1  SoL 108.9  ours/floor 1.9  headline x1.122 if halved`.
Route (scored PATH, log 922ddf4c): H-16f. Triton `_hm_align` runs first, then a fork: the hot half (wh5ds `wg_kernel<G,14>`)
goes on the fork stream and the cold half (Engine A v4cxs, E5XL arm) on the main stream. The join is followed by SGLang's
post-norm. Lanes not duplicated here: t256xl (GPU 4), xlncu (GPU 3), and hhxl (done, null).

**No node run, no build, no refusal.** The pre-registered rule measures on the node only when the lever is >= 3% headline.
It is not (section 3), so gpunode06 GPU 1 was never touched.

## 0. Pre-registration (from the brief)

2. Name the single biggest structural lever and size it in headline %. If it is >= 3%, measure its key unknown on gpunode06 GPU 1
   in consume-only mode where the H100 HBM wall would hide it.
3. Decision: >= 3% on the node and exact -> build it as a moe change in `.worktrees/m256` (`campaign/m256`). Otherwise write the
   anatomy and the null.

Sizing: headline = (row / (row - d))^(1/6). This is the plain geomean of 6 loads; halving the row gives x1.122, as in triage.
3% headline needs **d >= 44 us** (269.7 -> 225.7 us). Reference points: d 7 / 10 / 15 / 23 / 27 / 35 / 38 / 44 us gives
0.44 / 0.63 / 0.96 / 1.50 / 1.77 / 2.34 / 2.56 / 3.01%.

## 3. Lever

The largest slack is the **hot half** (~45 us above its ring floor). Its own floor (dequant-only 25.9 el/clk/SM over ~377M
hot weight elements) is ~52-55 us. Driving it there would be d = 38-44 us, i.e. **2.6-3.0% headline, but only as a ceiling**.
Every realizable form of it is already measured or modeled below the current kernel:
- **RS overlap** (dequant under an in-flight wgmma group, wait<1>, ping-pong, record pipelining): this is non-additive, and
  the dequant slows rather than hides (hothalf4: dov2 906 vs serial 787 cyc at N32; pp 891/1085). With 16 wgmma in flight,
  ptxas emits C7512 serialization at 168 regs.
- **SS / decoupled dequant warps** (forensics O3 / D3): it is smem-bound at 22 / 17.7 / 13.9 el/clk/SM for N = 16 / 64 / 128.
  The current RS stage already runs at 20.8 (N32) / 16.8 (N64). The streamed decoupled engine es1a lost to wg on the judge's
  own draws (log 922ddf4c: S/wg hot half x0.89 / 0.91 / 0.95 at p10/p50/p90).
- **Loop trims**: hhxl gave 1.0-1.6% judge-scaled on the hot half alone, i.e. ~0.2% headline (shelf).
- **Hot tail**: wg2, wgx, exact-N and the re-split are refuted. The hot w2 tail is already covered by the cold w2 under the
  fork (t256anat).

The **single biggest realizable structural lever** is therefore **the cold half's fixed cost and the kernel-boundary drains,
removed by one dependency-aware persistent schedule** (the panel's UFS, t256-designs angle 1). That means one launch after
the gather, one build_map, one fix-up round, and per-expert w13 -> w2 readiness counters, so a cold expert's w2 starts while
other experts' w13 still run. Hot items stay on wgmma.
- What it attacks: the cold fixed 27.4 us, plus the boundary drains of the w13 -> w2 hand-offs. It does **not** attack
  concurrency: overlap is zero-sum per SM (t256part).
- Size on today's row: the panel modeled -27 us mid and -41 us ceiling on the 304.7 us row. Since then the hot half lost
  ~23 us (wh5ds) and the cold half ~6% (E5XL), so the boundary and tail slack shrank with them. Scaled: **d ~ -15..-23 us =
  0.96-1.50% headline; ceiling ~-35 us = 2.3%.**
- Risks carried from the panel: C7512 when one function holds both the wgmma and mma.sync paths, role-switch interference
  (the K6 mixing was a no-go), and coarse hot items setting the tail.

Ranked alternatives (all below the bar):
- fused persistent cold engine alone: -8..-15 us = 0.5-0.96%;
- glue, fast multi-CTA align + `cp` ea_combine: -7..-10 us = 0.44-0.63%. `cp` is measured x1.016 on the node; the one-CTA
  align lost.
- A stack of all of them and the XL riders is ~-25..-40 us = 1.6-2.6%, and that is a multi-build stack, not one lever.

## 4. Verdict (pre-registered rule)

**NULL. No lever reaches 3% headline**, so no node measurement and no build. No `.worktrees/m256` and no `campaign/m256` were
created.
- The biggest realizable lever is the unified dependency-aware persistent schedule for the T=256 cold half and its
  boundaries: 0.96-1.50% (ceiling 2.3%).
- The biggest slack is the hot half's additive dequant + RS-wgmma stage: its 3.0% ceiling assumes the dequant-only floor,
  and every known form of reaching it is refuted (hothalf4, D3 smem model, es1a on the judge).

Reopen conditions:
1. A hot-half form that runs dequant concurrently with wgmma **without** the RF-port slowdown, measured >= x1.4 on the
   hothalf4 kstep rungs (consume-only, `[cyc]`). Only that moves the row by >= 38 us.
2. The UFS reopen rung from t256-designs angle 1: an nsys + gpu-metrics pass on the board bytes, showing judge-scaled
   per-SM idle >= 44 us inside the T=256 expert window.

Shelf riders for any T=256 stack: `cp` (x1.016 node), multi-CTA align (<= -7 us), and the t1xl / hhxl trims.
