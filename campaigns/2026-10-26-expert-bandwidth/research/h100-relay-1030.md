# H100 lane: relay reorder of the T=256 expert window (moe verify 32, bs 256), 2026-10-30 (gpunode06 GPU 1, 03:09Z-03:40Z)

Hypothesis (from h100-ufsrung-1030.md): the forked hybrid runs as a relay, cold w13 -> hot w13 -> cold w2 -> hot w2,
and the coarse hot w2 (wh5ds) runs last, so its ~8.3 us judge-scaled last-wave tail is uncovered. Reorder the relay so
the hot w2 runs before (or with the start of) the stream-K cold w2, and let the cold w2 fill the hot tail.

Pre-registered rule: a variant that saves >= 5 us judge-scaled on the window in all 3 replicates, with exact output,
gets built as a raced route on `.worktrees/relay`. Otherwise this is a null with numbers.

**Verdict: NULL. No variant clears 5 us.** The best one saves 0.5-1.9 us of graph time and 1.6-3.0 us of window
(judge-scaled ~0.5-2.8). That gain comes from launching the hot w2 without PDL, not from the reorder. Every variant that
actually moves the hot w2 ahead of the cold w2 is 6-14 us slower. No musespark branch or worktree was made, master is
untouched, and no okc was used.

## 1. Method

The kernels are untouched. A wrapper replaces `_hm_experts` only for the forked wgmma hybrid at T=256 with an XL cold
arm, outside prepare's fill and keep paths. It splits `_wh_pair` into gather / H13 / H2 and `_eae_pair` into C13 / C2,
and re-issues them in each mode's order. Mode 0 is master's own function. Prepare (races, canaries, route) runs entirely
in mode 0.

After prepare, a driver takes each judge-style T=256 draw (`_eb_ref_ids` 'normal'). It captures one kernel_fn graph per
mode and replays all modes 3 times each, in shuffled order, with a read flush before every replay. Each replay
records:
- the graph time (CUDA events, align -> combine);
- per-CTA `%globaltimer` / `%smid` for C13, C2, H13 and H2, which give the expert window and per-SM capacity idle
  (empty + 0.5 x half-occupied; ufsrung's accounting);
- `torch.equal` of the output against mode 0 on the same draw.

The table deltas are paired against mode 0 on the same draw and rep. The CI is a 95% bootstrap over draws.

Modes (cur = capture stream, which holds the cold half; fs = fork stream, which holds the hot half; hs = a
priority -1 stream; eg/eh = events after the gather / after H13):

| # | name | order |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | master | master's `_hm_experts` |
| 1 | ctl | the reimplementation in master's order (control) |
| 2 | coldfirst | (a) capture order: cold C13, C2 issued before the fork's G, H13, H2 |
| 3 | h2prio | (b) H2 on hs (prio -1) after `wait eh`. PDL is lost because the predecessor is on another stream |
| 4 | c2gate | (c) cur: C13, `wait eh`, C2. H2 and C2 are released together when H13 completes, H2 issued first |
| 5 | c2gate+prio | 4 with H2 on hs |
| 6 | hotfirst | cur waits on the gather (eg), so H13 is issued before C13 |
| 7 | hotfirst+gate | 6 + 4: the intended H13 -> C13 -> H2 -> C2 |
| 8 | hotfirst+prio | 6 with H2 on hs |
| 9 | h2nopdl | master order, H2 launched without PDL (r2/r3 only; separates 3's priority from its PDL-off) |
| 10 | h2prio3 | 3 on the highest-priority stream, -3 (r2/r3 only) |

## 3. Reading

**The reorder works mechanically and loses.** Modes 4 and 6-8 do put the hot w2 before the cold w2: H2 ends at
~234-239 us and C2 ends last in 360/360 replays. But the cold w2 does not fill the hot tail. It has a tail of its own,
and a longer one.
- ea4x K2 is a **static one-wave stream-K** grid: 264 CTAs at 2/SM, equal work per CTA. Its `tk2x` tickets are the
  fix-up's last-arriver counters, not a dynamic work queue.
- Launched behind H2, its CTAs land on SMs at the moment each H2 CTA ends. Equal work per CTA then keeps that landing
  stagger, and adds C2's own skew, at its end. The end spread is 29-38 us, against H2's 17 us on master.
- More important, the HBM-bound C2 then runs ~60-75 us with no compute-bound hot work beside it. On master the hot w2
  co-runs with the cold w2's last ~20 us, and the hot w13 with C13's tail.
- Capacity idle rises from 12.6-12.9 to 18-22 us/SM, and the window grows 4-13 us.

This is the same zero-sum relay mechanism as h100-fork-1029 Q1 (cold prio -1, x0.953-0.965), seen from the other side.
Whichever half ends alone pays its own tail, and the cold half's alone time costs more than the hot w2's 8 us.

**Capture order matters, and master's is the right one.**
- In coldfirst (2), the graph runs the cold w2 before the hot w13 (C2 121-215, H13 203-275). Branch issue order sets
  the block-scheduler FIFO, so the w13s overlap less: +10-12 us.
- In hotfirst (6-8), the hot w13 takes all 132 SMs first and the cold w13 fills its tail, but then C2 ends alone: +8-10.

**Priority alone cannot reorder this relay.** H2 cannot start until the whole hot w13 completes (a grid-level
dependency: PDL tail trigger or event). By then the one-wave C2 already holds every SM that the hot w13 freed from
~180 us on. So a priority -1 or -3 H2 (3, 10) or a gated C2 under priority (5) still runs H2 last, edges within ~4 us of
master. Delaying C2 with no high-priority H2 (4) leaves the H13 tail empty, which is the +12 us above.

**The only gain is H2 launched without PDL (3, 9, 10), and it is small.**
- Graph −0.5..−1.9 us; window −1.6..−3.0 us node.
- The CI excludes 0 only in r1 for mode 3 and in r2/r3 for mode 9.
- Capacity idle does not fall (13.4-13.8 vs 12.6-12.9), so this is not tail fill. H2 starts ~4 us earlier (251 vs 255)
  without the programmatic-serialization launch path.
- Caveat: the reimplemented master order (ctl) is itself +1.2-1.7 us slower than master's own function in all three
  runs. So against its true like-for-like control, h2prio is −1.9..−3.2 us graph.

**Decision (pre-registered): NULL.** The best variant is ~1-3 us judge-scaled at most, against a 5 us bar. That is
~0.1-0.2% headline on the 254.7 us row (headline = (row/(row−d))^(1/6)). No `.worktrees/relay` build, no nodejudge
validation, no okc.

What remains:
- **H2 without PDL** (master order, `wg_pdl` off for the T=256 w2 launch only) is a ~1 us graph rider. It is not
  standalone value, and it is too small to resolve on the judge. It is shelf material for a multi-build stack.
- Cheap and exact: a one-flag route that `_wg_pdl_stage` could race at T=256 against PDL-on.
- Its sign on the judge is unknown. The H200 judge's T=256 hot pair and launch path differ from gpunode06.

**Do not re-run:**
- relay reorders that put a static one-wave cold kernel last;
- capture-order or priority variants of the T=256 fork. Together with h100-fork-1029 Q1, every issue order, priority
  and gate on this relay has now been measured, and master's order is the best or tied.

The hot w2 tail (~8 us judge-scaled) can be removed only by:
- a cold w2 with dynamic, sub-CTA work issue that is still exact (fixed-order fix-up), so that it can fill whichever
  SMs free first;
- or a hot w2 whose last wave is balanced (the refuted wg2 / wgx / exact-N / re-split line).

Neither is an ordering change. The first falls under the fused / persistent cold engine line (UFS closed at ~11-12 us
fillable; m256 "fused persistent cold engine" on the shelf).

## 5. Method notes for reuse

- `relay_instr.py`'s `_rl_run` is a clean host-side decomposition of the forked hybrid: G / H13 / H2 / C13 / C2 as
  closures. Any stream, event or priority arrangement can be A/B'd in one process on identical draws, with a bitwise
  check, at about 1 minute of node time per 40 draws x 11 modes x 3 reps.
- Fix before reuse: the raw-JSON path should use the probe's own `out/<probe>` directory, not the first
  `out/nodejudge-*`.
- The ctl-vs-master offset (+1.2-1.7 us graph, window ~0) is unexplained. It may be allocation layout: master
  allocates an unused `cache2` and orders its allocations differently. Pair new arms against ctl as well as master.
